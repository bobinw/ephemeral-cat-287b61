An Outlaw in the Forest
When Robin was a young man, he went to an archery contest.
On the road, some of the king's forest guards laughed at him.
They dared him to shoot a deer, though hunting there was against the law.
Robin accepted, and his arrow brought the deer down.
Instead of paying their bet, the guards threatened him.
One shot an arrow as Robin walked away.
Robin fired back and killed the man.
He could not undo what had happened.
Wanted by the law, he hid in Sherwood Forest.
Other men who had suffered under powerful people joined him.
They became a band of outlaws, taking money from rich travelers and helping people in need.
Their life offered freedom, but it also brought danger and difficult choices.

A Friend on the Bridge
One day, Robin met a very tall stranger on a narrow bridge.
Neither man would step aside to let the other pass.
Robin had his bow, but the stranger carried only a staff.
Using a bow would give Robin an unfair advantage.
He cut a staff, and they fought on equal terms.
The stranger knocked Robin into the stream.
Robin came up wet, angry for a moment, and then laughing.
He could recognize skill even when it defeated him.
The stranger's name was John Little.
Robin's band called this huge man Little John.
He became one of Robin's closest friends.
Their friendship began when both men learned that pride was not everything.

The Tinker and the Sheriff
The Sheriff of Nottingham wanted Robin captured.
A traveling tinker carried an order to arrest the outlaw.
Robin met him without saying who he was.
They drank together, and the tinker talked proudly about his task.
While the man slept, Robin took the order away.
The tinker later found him, and their staffs crashed together.
This time, Robin needed Little John's help.
The tinker discovered that the forest men were harder to defeat than he had expected.
Robin invited him to share their meal rather than continue the fight.
The man decided to join them.
Robin gained a useful friend by ending a quarrel.
But the sheriff still waited outside the forest, looking for a weakness.

Saving Will Stutely
One of Robin's men, Will Stutely, was caught by the sheriff's guards.
The sheriff planned to hang him in public.
Robin's band could stay hidden and remain safe.
Instead, they went to help their friend.
They entered the town in disguise and mixed with the crowd.
Little John reached Will and spoke quietly to him.
Then he struck the guards and gave Will a weapon.
Robin's men rushed forward before the sheriff could understand their plan.
Arrows held the other guards back while Will escaped.
The band returned to the green shelter of Sherwood.
Will knew that his friends had risked their lives for him.
Freedom meant little if they abandoned one another whenever danger came.

A Butcher's Bargain
Robin bought a butcher's meat, cart, and clothes.
Disguised as a butcher, he went to the market in Nottingham.
He sold meat so cheaply that the other butchers wondered about him.
They guessed that he was a rich young man who knew nothing about business.
At a meal, Robin told the sheriff that he had many animals for sale.
Hoping for an easy profit, the sheriff followed him into the forest.
Robin showed him deer instead of farm animals.
Then the hidden forest men appeared.
The sheriff understood that the bargain had been a trap.
Robin made him pay for a meal and let him go.
The trick made the band laugh, but it made the sheriff more determined.
Humiliating an enemy did not make that enemy disappear.

An Arrow Made of Gold
The sheriff offered a golden arrow as the prize in an archery contest.
He hoped Robin's love of archery would draw him out of hiding.
Robin knew the danger, but he went in disguise.
His friends spread through the crowd to help if needed.
The contestants shot at smaller and smaller targets.
Robin's final arrow won the prize.
The sheriff did not recognize the poor-looking man before him.
Robin took the golden arrow and slipped away.
Later, a message revealed who had really won.
The band enjoyed another victory, yet their safety remained uncertain.
Robin could hit a tiny mark across a field.
He could not make the law forget his name.

A Knight Who Needed Help
One day, a poor-looking knight came through the forest.
His name was Sir Richard, and he owed a large sum to a rich church leader.
He had borrowed it to help his son after serious trouble.
If he could not pay, he would lose his land.
Robin listened instead of treating him like every rich traveler.
He lent the knight money and sent Little John with him.
The church leader had expected to gain the land cheaply.
He was disappointed when the knight arrived with payment.
Later, Sir Richard returned with money and gifts for the forest men.
Robin's kindness had created a friendship stronger than fear.
The knight also helped shelter the band when danger came.
Giving help could build a future that taking by force never could.

A Singer and a Promise
Robin met a young singer called Allan a Dale.
Allan loved a woman who was being forced to marry someone else.
Robin decided to help them choose their own future.
He entered the church with his men and interrupted the wedding.
The woman chose Allan, and they were married with the band's support.
The forest now held songs as well as weapons.
Robin also met Friar Tuck, a strong man with a quick temper.
After a rough meeting and a fight, they became friends.
The band was made of people with different skills and different faults.
Robin did not win their loyalty by being perfect.
He shared their meals, their dangers, and their joy.
Their friendship gave meaning to a life outside ordinary society.

A King in Disguise
King Richard heard stories about Robin and wanted to meet him.
He entered the forest dressed as a churchman.
Robin's men stopped him and invited him to eat.
The king watched their games and saw their skill with bows.
He also heard Robin speak with respect about King Richard.
At last, the visitor showed who he was.
Robin knelt before him, and the king pardoned the band.
Robin agreed to enter the king's service.
For a time, he lived away from Sherwood.
But life at court could not replace the forest and his friends.
He eventually returned to the greenwood.
Forgiveness had changed his position, but not everything that mattered to him.

The Last Arrow
Years passed, and King Richard died.
Under another king, Robin faced danger again.
He grew older and became ill.
He went to a religious house at Kirklees for treatment.
A woman there betrayed him, and the treatment left him dying.
Robin blew his horn, and Little John came to his side.
His friend wanted revenge, but Robin would not allow it.
He asked for his bow and one last arrow.
The arrow flew out through the window.
Robin asked to be buried where it landed.
Little John kept that final promise.
The adventures ended, but stories of the forest friends continued to travel far beyond Sherwood.
