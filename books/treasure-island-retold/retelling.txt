The Sailor at the Inn
My name is Jim Hawkins, and this is the story of how I found treasure.
My parents kept a small inn near the sea.
One day, an old sailor arrived with a heavy sea chest.
He called himself the captain, but his real name was Billy Bones.
He drank too much and sang loud songs about pirates.
He paid me to watch for a sailor with one leg.
I began to see that man in my dreams.
Billy frightened our guests, but Doctor Livesey was not afraid of him.
The doctor warned him that too much drink would kill him.
Then a stranger called Black Dog came to the inn.
He and Billy fought, and Black Dog ran away.
Billy fell ill, and I understood that his past had found him.

The Map in the Chest
My father died while Billy was staying with us.
My mother and I were grieving, and we needed money to keep the inn.
One evening, a blind man made me lead him to Billy.
He put a small piece of paper into Billy's hand.
It was the black spot, a warning from the pirates.
Billy tried to stand up, but he fell and died.
Mother opened his chest to take the money he owed us.
She refused to take more, even though we were in danger.
I took a packet of papers, and we escaped before the pirates arrived.
Later, I showed the papers to the doctor and Squire Trelawney.
Inside was a map showing where Captain Flint had buried his gold.
The squire promised to buy a ship, and suddenly my life became an adventure.

A Friendly Man with One Leg
Our ship was called the Hispaniola.
In Bristol, the squire hired a cook named Long John Silver.
Silver had one leg, a warm smile, and a parrot on his shoulder.
His parrot shouted, "Pieces of eight!"
I remembered Billy's warning, but Silver seemed too kind to be dangerous.
He told me about ships and treated me like a friend.
He also helped the squire find sailors for our voyage.
Captain Smollett did not like the way the voyage had been planned.
The squire had talked freely about the treasure, and everyone knew our secret.
The captain moved the guns and powder to a safer place on the ship.
I thought he was too strict, while Silver seemed wonderful.
I had not yet learned how easily a friendly face can hide a dangerous plan.

Inside the Apple Barrel
One night, I climbed into a barrel to find an apple.
Before I could climb out, Silver sat down beside it.
He began talking to two sailors, and I stayed very still.
Silver had sailed with Captain Flint, and many of our sailors were pirates.
They planned to let the captain find the treasure first.
Then they would take the ship and kill anyone who stood in their way.
The man I trusted was quietly planning to destroy us.
My heart beat hard, but I waited until the men had left.
I told the doctor, the squire, and the captain what I had heard.
There were far more pirates than loyal men on board.
We agreed to smile, keep our secret, and wait for a chance to act.
Now every friendly word from Silver made me feel cold.

The Man in the Trees
When we reached the island, the captain let some sailors go ashore.
I slipped into their boat because I wanted to see the island for myself.
Once we landed, I ran into the trees.
I heard Silver arguing with Tom, an honest sailor.
Tom refused to join the pirates, and Silver killed him.
I ran away in fear, no longer doubting what Silver could do.
Then a thin man appeared among the trees and spoke my name.
He was Ben Gunn, a sailor left on the island three years before.
He had survived alone, but he longed to go home and eat cheese again.
Ben knew Silver, and he knew a great deal about Flint's treasure.
He offered to help if the doctor would take him home.
Before we parted, he told me where he had hidden a little boat.

The Wooden Fort
While I was exploring, the doctor and our loyal men left the ship.
They carried food and guns to an old wooden fort with fresh water.
I found them there and told the doctor about Ben Gunn.
The next morning, Silver arrived carrying a white flag.
He offered to let us live if we gave him the treasure map.
Captain Smollett refused to trust a promise from a man who had betrayed us.
Silver left in anger, and soon the pirates attacked.
We fought from behind the wooden walls.
We held the fort, but several men died and the captain was badly hurt.
The island no longer felt like a place for a wonderful adventure.
People were paying for the treasure with their lives.
I wanted to help, but I was about to make another dangerous choice alone.

A Small Boat at Night
The fort was hot, and I grew tired of waiting.
Without asking anyone, I took some food and slipped away.
I found Ben Gunn's little boat beneath a white rock.
It was light and round, and it was difficult to control.
After dark, I pushed it into the sea and moved towards the Hispaniola.
Only two pirates were on the ship, and they were drinking and fighting.
I cut the rope that held the ship in place.
The ship began to drift, and I hoped the pirates would lose it.
But the sea also carried my small boat away from shore.
Waves rose around me, and I could not make the boat obey.
I was alone in the dark because I had refused to ask for help.
At last I fell asleep, too tired to fight the sea any longer.

The Ship Is Mine
In the morning, I saw the Hispaniola drifting nearby.
I managed to climb on board before my little boat sank.
One pirate was dead, and the other, Israel Hands, was badly hurt.
I took down the pirate flag and said that the ship was mine.
Hands offered to help me sail into the sheltered North Inlet.
I needed his knowledge, but I watched him carefully.
While I was busy with the ship, he hid a knife.
When we reached shallow water, he suddenly came after me.
I climbed up the mast, and he threw his knife into my shoulder.
I fired my pistol, and Hands fell into the sea.
Shaking with fear, I freed myself and covered the wound.
I had saved the ship, but I did not feel like a happy hero.

Among the Pirates
That night, I walked back to the fort, hoping to find my friends.
The place was dark, and I stepped inside quietly.
Suddenly, Silver's parrot cried out above my head.
The pirates had taken the fort, and I was their prisoner.
Silver told me that the doctor had given them the fort and the map.
I could not understand why my friends had done this.
The pirates wanted to kill me, but Silver stood between us.
He hoped I would speak for him if he ever faced a trial.
I told him that I had heard his plan and hidden the ship.
His men grew angry and gave him another black spot.
But Silver showed them the map and won back their trust.
He had protected me, yet I knew he was also protecting himself.

A Promise to Keep
The next morning, Doctor Livesey came to treat the sick pirates.
He helped them even though they were his enemies.
When he saw me, he looked both relieved and angry.
I had left my friends without warning and put myself in danger.
I told him that I was sorry and that the ship was safe in the North Inlet.
The doctor wanted me to run away with him.
But I had promised Silver that I would not escape.
I did not want to become a person whose words meant nothing.
The doctor listened, although it was hard for him to leave me there.
He asked Silver to keep me safe and warned him to be careful at the treasure.
The doctor seemed to know something that neither of us understood.
As he walked away, I hoped that keeping my promise was not a terrible mistake.

The Empty Treasure Pit
Silver led the pirates into the forest, and I walked beside him.
We found a skeleton with an arm pointing towards the treasure.
Flint had left it there as a sign, and the men were afraid.
Then a strange voice rose from the trees, singing Flint's old song.
Some pirates thought that a dead man had come back.
Silver kept them moving, but even he looked worried.
At last we reached the place marked on the map.
The treasure pit was empty, with only a few broken boards inside.
The pirates turned on Silver, believing that he had tricked them.
Before they could attack us, shots came from the trees.
The doctor, Ben Gunn, and our friend Gray rushed out and saved us.
Ben had found and moved the gold long before we arrived, and the strange voice was his.

What We Took Home
Ben led us to his cave, where the gold lay in great piles.
The doctor had learned his secret and given the pirates a useless map.
For days, we carried the heavy treasure to our ship.
Silver worked hard beside us, always watching for his next chance.
We left three surviving pirates on the island with food, tools, and medicine.
Then we sailed away from the place where so many people had died.
At a port on our way home, Silver escaped with a bag of money.
He had saved my life, but that did not erase the cruel things he had done.
We each received a share of the treasure, and I returned to my mother.
The money made our lives easier, but it could not bring back the people we had lost.
Sometimes I still hear the sea in my dreams and the parrot crying for silver coins.
I have no wish to return to Treasure Island, because some things are worth more than gold.
