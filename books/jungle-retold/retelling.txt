A Child Among Wolves
One evening, a small human child walked into a wolf family's cave.
The tiger Shere Khan wanted the child, but Mother Wolf refused to give him up.
She named him Mowgli and raised him beside her own cubs.
The wolf pack met to decide whether he could stay.
Baloo the bear spoke for him, and Bagheera the black panther offered a bull as payment.
The pack accepted the child.
But Shere Khan waited for a chance to turn the wolves against him.
Mowgli's safety depended on more than the strength of his own arms.

Learning the Jungle's Law
Baloo taught Mowgli the rules that helped different animals live together.
He learned how to ask for food and how to speak to creatures unlike himself.
Sometimes the lessons felt dull, and Baloo could be a harsh teacher.
Bagheera reminded him that the boy needed care as well as discipline.
Mowgli was clever, but being clever did not mean knowing everything.
He liked the monkeys because they praised him and promised endless freedom.
The monkeys had no lasting rules, and they quickly forgot their promises.
His teachers warned him, but he did not yet understand why.

The Monkeys' Empty Promises
The monkeys carried Mowgli away through the tops of the trees.
He asked a bird to tell Baloo and Bagheera where he was going.
That message used a lesson he had thought unimportant.
The monkeys took him to ruined buildings and talked about great plans.
But they could not even agree to bring him food.
Baloo and Bagheera asked Kaa, the great snake, for help.
Together they fought through the monkeys and freed Mowgli.
He learned that friendship was shown by actions, not just exciting words.

The Red Flower
As Mowgli grew, some wolves began listening to Shere Khan.
Their old leader, Akela, grew weaker, and the pack turned against him too.
Bagheera told Mowgli to bring fire from the human village.
The animals feared fire and called it the Red Flower.
At the pack's meeting, Mowgli used it to protect himself and Akela.
He drove Shere Khan away, but he could not make the pack love him.
He cried as he said goodbye to the family that had raised him.
Being different hurt most when it separated him from those he loved.

Between Two Worlds
Mowgli entered a village, where a woman called Messua cared for him.
He learned human words, clothes, and work.
Yet many village customs made little sense to him.
He became a keeper of buffaloes and stayed in touch with his wolf brother.
His brother warned him that Shere Khan was hunting him again.
With help from Akela and the young wolf, Mowgli divided the buffaloes into two groups.
They drove the animals into a narrow valley from both ends.
Trapped between them, Shere Khan was killed.

Choosing His Own Company
A village hunter tried to claim Mowgli's victory as his own.
When Mowgli refused, the man spread frightening stories about him.
The villagers drove him away, though Messua tried to protect him.
Mowgli returned to the jungle with the tiger's skin.
The wolves wanted him back, but he would not simply obey the pack again.
He chose to hunt with his four wolf brothers.
He belonged neither wholly to the village nor wholly to the pack.
For now, he would make his way beside companions whose loyalty he knew.
