A Prince Above the City
High above a city stood a statue called the Happy Prince.
Gold covered him, blue stones formed his eyes, and a red stone shone on his sword.
People admired his beauty as they passed.
A swallow stopped beneath the statue on its way to Egypt.
Drops fell on it, though the sky was clear.
The bird looked up and saw the Prince crying.
While alive, he had lived behind palace walls and known little sorrow.
Now he could see the suffering hidden throughout his city.

The First Gift
In a poor house, a woman was sewing while her sick child asked for something to drink.
She had no money to buy what he needed.
The Prince asked the swallow to take the red stone from his sword to her.
The bird wanted to leave, but agreed to stay one night.
It carried the stone into the house and cooled the child's face with its wings.
Returning to the Prince, the swallow felt warm despite the cold.
The Prince said it was because the bird had done something kind.
The swallow had discovered a feeling its journey had not given it before.

The Price of Seeing
The next night, the Prince saw a young writer too cold and hungry to finish his work.
He asked the swallow to take one of his blue eyes to him.
The bird protested, but the Prince insisted.
On another night, a little girl lost the matches she was selling.
She feared going home without money.
The Prince gave her his other eye.
Now he could no longer see the city at all.
The swallow decided to remain with him instead of flying south.

Gold for Those Who Needed It
The bird flew through the city and described what it saw.
It saw rich people enjoying comfort while children went hungry.
The Prince asked it to remove the gold covering his body.
Piece by piece, the swallow carried it to people who needed food.
The children began to laugh and play again.
But the statue became dull, and the weather grew colder.
The swallow was growing weak.
It stayed because its friendship mattered more to it than its own journey.

A Broken Heart
At last, the swallow knew it was dying.
It kissed the Prince goodbye and fell at his feet.
Inside the statue, the lead heart broke into two pieces.
The city's leaders saw the shabby statue and decided it was useless.
They pulled it down and melted the metal.
But the broken heart would not melt.
It was thrown onto a rubbish heap beside the dead bird.
The city had measured their worth by what remained on the outside.

The Most Precious Things
In the story's final scene, God asked an angel for the city's two most precious things.
The angel brought the broken heart and the dead swallow.
These were the things the city's leaders had thrown away.
God welcomed the bird to sing in his garden and the Prince to praise him.
Their love had been seen, even though the city had failed to see it.
The Prince had become less beautiful while making other lives better.
The swallow had lost its distant destination but found a friend.
The ending asks us what is truly precious, and how we decide.
