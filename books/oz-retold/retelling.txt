The House in the Wind
Dorothy lived in Kansas with Aunt Em, Uncle Henry, and her little dog, Toto.
One day, a powerful storm lifted their house into the sky.
When it landed, Dorothy found herself in the strange country of Oz.
The house had fallen on the Wicked Witch of the East, freeing the people she ruled.
The good Witch of the North gave Dorothy the dead witch's silver shoes.
She could not send Dorothy home, but she knew someone who might help.
Dorothy must follow the yellow brick road to the great Wizard of Oz.
The witch kissed her forehead to protect her on the journey.

Friends Who Thought They Lacked Something
Along the road, Dorothy found a Scarecrow hanging in a field.
He wanted a brain, so he joined her journey.
They freed a Tin Woodman whose metal body had become stiff with rust.
He once had a human body, but an evil spell had taken it away piece by piece.
He wanted a heart so that he could love again.
A Lion joined them because he wanted courage.
Yet the Scarecrow made clever plans, the Tin Woodman cared for living things, and the Lion protected his friends.
They were already showing qualities they believed they did not have.

The Price of Help
After crossing forests and rivers, they reached the Emerald City.
The Wizard appeared differently to each visitor, frightening and powerful.
He promised help only if they destroyed the Wicked Witch of the West.
The friends were afraid, but they went together.
The witch sent animals and soldiers against them.
Then she used a magic golden cap to command the Winged Monkeys.
The monkeys captured Dorothy and the Lion, and damaged their friends.
Dorothy's protective kiss kept the witch from killing her.

Water and a Golden Cap
The witch made Dorothy work and tried to steal her silver shoes.
When she took one shoe, Dorothy angrily threw water over her.
To Dorothy's surprise, the witch melted away.
The people were free, and they helped repair the Scarecrow and the Tin Woodman.
Dorothy found the golden cap, which allowed its owner three commands to the monkeys.
She used it to bring the friends back to the Emerald City.
Now they expected the Wizard to keep his promise.
Instead, they discovered another secret.

The Man Behind the Curtain
Toto pulled away a curtain and revealed an ordinary man.
The Wizard used tricks to make himself seem powerful.
He had arrived in Oz by balloon and could not do real magic.
He gave the Scarecrow a brain made of simple materials, the Tin Woodman a silk heart, and the Lion a drink called courage.
These gifts helped them believe in qualities they had already shown.
He built a balloon to take Dorothy home, but it rose before she could climb aboard.
Dorothy was left behind, terribly disappointed.
Her friends agreed to seek Glinda, the good Witch of the South.

The Road Home
They faced more dangers together and finally reached Glinda.
Glinda explained that Dorothy's silver shoes could carry her home.
Dorothy had worn the answer throughout her journey.
But without the journey, she would never have met her friends or discovered their strength.
Glinda used the remaining commands of the golden cap to help the friends reach their new homes.
Then she freed the Winged Monkeys from further service.
Dorothy said goodbye, tapped her heels three times, and asked to go to Kansas.
She arrived home with Toto and ran into Aunt Em's arms.
