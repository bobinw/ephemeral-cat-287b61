# 1. The Sea Beyond the Window | 窗外的大海
My name is Robinson Crusoe, and I was born in York, where my father hoped I would lead a quiet, useful life. || 我叫鲁滨逊·克鲁索，出生在约克；父亲希望我过一种平静而有用的生活。
He had worked hard for his comfort and could not understand why his youngest son wished to throw it away. || 他辛苦工作才换来安稳，不明白小儿子为什么想把它抛开。
I understood his reasons well enough, but whenever I imagined the sea, his comfortable rooms seemed to grow smaller. || 他的道理我不是不懂，可每当想到大海，家里舒适的房间便仿佛变小了。
One morning he called me to his bedside, for pain in his feet kept him from walking. || 一天早晨，他把我叫到床边；双脚的疼痛让他无法走动。
He spoke of men who lost everything through hunger, and others who lost their peace through wanting too much. || 他谈起那些因贫困而失去一切的人，也谈起那些因贪求太多而失去安宁的人。
Between those dangers, he believed, lay the happiest place a man could find. || 他认为，两种危险之间，才是人能找到的最幸福的位置。
When he remembered my brother, who had died in a distant war, his voice broke and tears ran down his face. || 想到我那个死于远方战争的哥哥，他声音哽咽，眼泪流了下来。
I promised myself that I would stay, and for several days the promise seemed quite strong. || 我向自己保证留下来；一连几天，这个决心看起来都十分坚定。
Then the wish returned, as quietly as water finding its way through a wall. || 随后，那个愿望又回来了，像水悄悄渗过墙缝。
In Hull, a friend offered me a passage to London on his father's ship, and I left without telling my parents. || 在赫尔，一个朋友邀请我搭乘他父亲的船去伦敦，我没告诉父母就走了。
I had hardly begun to enjoy my freedom when the wind rose and the deck started moving beneath my feet. || 我还没来得及好好享受自由，风就大了起来，甲板在脚下摇晃。
Sick, frightened, and certain that every wave would swallow us, I promised to go home if I survived. || 我又晕船又害怕，觉得每一道浪都会吞掉我们，发誓只要活下来就回家。
The next evening brought a smooth sea, golden light, and a friend who laughed at what I had called a storm. || 第二天傍晚，大海平静了，阳光金灿灿的，我的朋友还笑我把那点风浪叫作暴风雨。
By the time we had shared a drink, my promises were disappearing almost as quickly as my sickness. || 喝过酒后，我的誓言几乎和晕船的不适一样迅速地消失了。
A worse storm followed, and this time even the sailors looked at one another without smiling. || 更猛烈的风暴接踵而来，这一次，连水手们相互对视时也不再带着笑容。
We worked at the pumps while water entered faster than our tired arms could send it out. || 我们不停地抽水，可疲惫的双臂把水排出去的速度，赶不上它涌进来的速度。
Another vessel sent a boat, and we escaped before our ship sank beneath the waves. || 另一艘船派来小艇，我们及时逃了出来，随后自己的船沉入波涛。
The captain warned me to return home, saying plainly that the sea was not meant for me. || 船长直截了当地劝我回家，说大海不适合我。
Yet I was more ashamed of being laughed at for coming back than of having made a foolish choice. || 然而，相比于做出了愚蠢选择，我更羞于回家后被人笑话。
I went on to London, carrying the memory of the storm but not the lesson I had promised to learn. || 我继续去了伦敦，带着风暴的记忆，却没带上自己答应吸取的教训。

# 2. Freedom Bought and Sold | 买卖中的自由
In London I met a captain who treated me kindly and taught me something of trade and navigation. || 在伦敦，我遇到一位友善的船长，他教给我一些贸易和航海的知识。
A voyage to the African coast brought me a profit, and success made my father's warnings sound very far away. || 去非洲海岸的一次航行让我赚到了钱，成功使父亲的警告听起来十分遥远。
The captain died, but I returned to sea, expecting another profitable journey instead of the disaster that awaited us. || 后来船长去世了，我却再次出海，期待又一次赚钱的旅程，不知道灾难正在等着我们。
Our ship was attacked, and after a hard fight I was taken prisoner and made to work for my captor. || 我们的船遭到袭击，激战过后，我被俘，被迫为俘获我的人干活。
For two years I belonged, in his eyes, to him, and every plan of escape seemed to end at a locked door. || 两年间，在他眼里我属于他；每一个逃跑计划，似乎都止于一道锁上的门。
At last he sent me fishing in a boat with another man and a boy called Xury. || 终于有一天，他派我和另一个男人、一个叫佐立的男孩乘艇捕鱼。
I secretly put food, water, and guns aboard, then waited until we were well away from shore. || 我偷偷把食物、水和枪装上船，等着小艇远离岸边。
I pushed the man into the sea and warned him to swim back instead of trying to follow us. || 我把那个男人推入海里，警告他游回岸边，不要试图追我们。
Xury agreed to come with me, and we sailed south along a coast that neither of us knew well. || 佐立愿意跟我走，我们沿着一片双方都不熟悉的海岸向南航行。
We watched the shore for fresh water, listened for animals after dark, and counted our food before every meal. || 我们沿岸寻找淡水，天黑后留意野兽的声音，每顿饭前都清点食物。
When people on the shore brought us food, we answered with gestures, for no shared language was available. || 岸上的人给我们送来食物时，我们用手势回应，因为彼此语言不通。
Once we killed a great animal near the water, but its meat was useless to us, and I regretted the powder wasted. || 有一次，我们在水边打死一只大野兽，可它的肉不能供我们食用，我懊恼浪费了火药。
Xury worked beside me to remove its skin, which later served as bedding in our little boat. || 佐立与我一起剥下兽皮，后来把它当作小艇上的铺盖。
After many anxious days, a Portuguese ship saw our signals and took us aboard. || 经过许多个提心吊胆的日子，一艘葡萄牙船看到了我们的信号，把我们接上船。
Its captain refused to charge me for saving my life, though he bought the boat and several of our possessions. || 船长不肯为救命收钱，不过他买下了小艇和我们的一些物品。
He also offered to buy Xury, and I hesitated at selling the liberty of the boy who had helped me gain mine. || 他还提出买下佐立；想到这个男孩帮我获得自由，我却要卖掉他的自由，我犹豫了。
The captain promised to free him after ten years if he became a Christian, and Xury agreed to go. || 船长答应，如果佐立成为基督徒，十年后就给他自由；佐立同意了。
I accepted the bargain and went on to Brazil, where I began growing sugar and tobacco. || 我接受了这笔交易，随后来到巴西，开始种植甘蔗和烟草。
My land prospered, yet I often wished for Xury's help and complained of having too much work for one man. || 我的土地逐渐兴旺，可我常希望佐立还能来帮忙，抱怨一个人要做的事太多。
When neighboring planters proposed a voyage to buy enslaved Africans for their farms, I agreed to sail with them. || 当邻近的种植园主提议出海购买被奴役的非洲人来种田时，我同意参加航行。
I already had the means of a settled life, but again I left them behind for the promise of more. || 我已经有条件过安稳生活，却又一次为了得到更多而把它抛在身后。

# 3. The Shore After the Storm | 风暴后的海岸
We sailed from Brazil with trading goods aboard, expecting to return with a cargo that would make our fortunes. || 我们从巴西出发，带着用于交易的货物，期待满载归来，发上一笔财。
Instead, storm winds drove us far from our course, and the sea rose against us day after day. || 然而，暴风把我们吹得远离航线，海浪一天又一天向我们扑来。
At last our ship struck a sandbank, and the waves began breaking over her as if she were already part of the shore. || 最后，船撞上了沙洲，海浪越过船身，仿佛它已经成了海岸的一部分。
We lowered a boat and crowded into it, leaving behind the vessel that had carried all our hopes. || 我们放下小艇，挤了进去，把那艘载着全部希望的船留在身后。
A great wave overturned us, and for a time I knew nothing except water, darkness, and the desperate need for air. || 一个巨浪掀翻了小艇，一时间，我只知道身边是水和黑暗，自己急需空气。
When my feet touched ground, another wave lifted me and threw me forward before I could stand. || 双脚刚碰到地面，我还没站稳，另一道浪就把我卷起，向前抛去。
I held my breath, struggled towards the land, and was struck against a rock hard enough to leave me helpless. || 我屏住呼吸，拼命挣向岸边，却撞上岩石，疼得一时动弹不得。
A final rush of water carried me higher, and I dragged myself beyond its reach on my hands and knees. || 最后一股水流把我推到更高处，我用手脚撑着身子，爬到它够不到的地方。
For a while I lay looking at the sky, unable to believe that I was still alive. || 我躺着望向天空，许久都无法相信自己还活着。
Then I stood and searched the water for the other men, calling until my throat hurt. || 随后我站起来，在水面上寻找其他人，喊得喉咙发疼。
The sea returned a few caps and shoes, but no answering voice, and I understood that I was alone. || 海水送回了几顶帽子和几只鞋，却没有任何回应的声音，我明白自己孤身一人了。
My gratitude for being saved gave way to terror when I examined what I had brought ashore. || 当我检查自己带上岸的东西时，获救的感激变成了恐惧。
There was a knife, a little tobacco, and a pipe, but no food, no shelter, and no companion. || 我只有一把刀、少许烟草和一个烟斗，没有食物，没有住所，也没有同伴。
I did not know whether the island held dangerous animals, or whether anyone would ever search for us there. || 我不知道岛上有没有危险的野兽，也不知道是否会有人来这里寻找我们。
Thirst drove me inland until I found fresh water, and that small discovery gave me courage to take another step. || 口渴迫使我向内陆走去，直到找到淡水；这个小发现给了我继续走下一步的勇气。
For the night I climbed into a thick tree and arranged myself among its branches so that sleep would not drop me. || 到了夜里，我爬上一棵茂密的大树，在树枝间安顿好，免得睡着后跌下来。
I cut a short stick for protection, though it seemed a poor answer to every danger I could imagine. || 我削了一根短棍防身，尽管面对自己想象的种种危险，它显得十分无力。
Exhaustion did what confidence could not, and I slept deeply above the dark ground. || 疲惫完成了信心做不到的事：我在漆黑的地面上方沉沉睡去。
In the morning the storm had passed, and I saw that the wreck had moved closer to the island. || 早晨，风暴过去了，我发现失事的船被推到了离岛更近的地方。
If anything remained dry inside her, I might yet have something better than a stick with which to begin my life. || 如果船里还有没被水浸坏的东西，我或许还能找到比一根棍子更好的东西，开始新的生活。

# 4. What Was Worth Saving | 什么值得抢救
At low tide I swam to the ship, but her smooth side offered no place for my hands. || 退潮时，我游到了船旁，可光滑的船身没有能让双手抓住的地方。
I circled her until I found a hanging rope, climbed aboard, and discovered that some stores had escaped the water. || 我绕着船找，终于发现一根垂下的绳索，爬上船后发现一些物资没有被水浸坏。
I ate biscuits greedily before beginning work, for a starving man makes a poor carpenter. || 开始干活前，我狼吞虎咽地吃了些饼干，因为饥饿的人做不好木工活。
The ship's boat was gone, so I tied spare pieces of timber together into a raft. || 船上的小艇已不见了，于是我把备用木料绑起来，做成木筏。
It had no proper sides and no sail, but I hoped the quiet water would carry it safely towards shore. || 木筏没有像样的船舷，也没有帆，但我希望平静的水能把它安全送到岸边。
I loaded food, clothes, guns, and dry powder, then found a chest containing the carpenter's tools. || 我装上食物、衣物、枪和干火药，接着找到了装着木工工具的箱子。
No chest of gold could have pleased me more than those axes, saws, and nails. || 即使一箱黄金，也不会比那些斧头、锯子和钉子更让我高兴。
Getting the raft ashore was harder than building it, for the tide turned it wherever it wished. || 把木筏弄上岸比造它更难，潮水想让它朝哪边转，它就朝哪边转。
Once one end caught on a shallow place, and the other dipped low enough to threaten my whole cargo. || 有一次，一端卡在浅处，另一端低低沉下，眼看整筏货物都要落水。
I leaned against the timber, held it steady with all my strength, and waited for the water to rise. || 我靠着木料，用全身力气稳住它，等着水位上涨。
When at last I landed, I carried every item above the tide line and looked again towards the wreck. || 终于靠岸后，我把每一样东西搬到潮水够不到的地方，又望向失事的船。
A new storm could break her apart at any moment, and whatever I left would then belong to the sea. || 一场新风暴随时可能把它击碎，留下的任何东西都会归大海所有。
I returned again and again, making smaller loads after an overloaded raft overturned and lost much of its iron. || 我一次又一次返回船上；有回木筏因装得过多而翻覆，损失了许多铁器，此后我减少了每次的载重。
The ship's dog reached me by swimming, and I also brought two cats ashore, together with more bedding and rope. || 船上的狗自己游到了我身边，我还把两只猫带上岸，又搬来更多铺盖和绳索。
The animals could not answer my questions, but the sound of another living creature was a comfort. || 动物不能回答我的问题，可听到另一个活物发出声音，仍让我感到安慰。
On one visit I found coins in a drawer and laughed at the use I could possibly make of them. || 有一次，我在抽屉里找到了钱币，不禁笑起来：在这里，它们究竟能有什么用？
A single good knife was worth more to me than all that silver, with no shop and no person to pay. || 这里没有商店，也没有可以付钱给的人，一把好刀比那一堆银币更有价值。
Nevertheless, I wrapped the money in canvas and took it away with the rest. || 尽管如此，我还是用帆布裹好钱币，和其他东西一起带走了。
Before another journey could begin, the wind rose, rain darkened the shore, and I spent the night inside my tent. || 在下一次返回船上之前，风起了，雨使海岸变得昏暗，我在帐篷里熬过了这一夜。
At dawn the ship was gone, and the tools beside me were all that remained of the world that had brought me there. || 天亮时，船不见了；把我送到这里的那个世界，只剩下身边这些工具。

# 5. A Castle for One Man | 一个人的城堡
I needed a home that would stay dry in the rain and allow me to see the sea without being easily seen myself. || 我需要一个下雨时仍然干燥、既能看到大海又不易被别人发现的住处。
After searching, I chose a flat piece of ground below a rocky hill, with a hollow behind it. || 寻找一番后，我选中一座石山下的平地，后面有一个凹洞。
I drove strong stakes into the earth in two rows, filling the space between them to make a wall. || 我把结实的木桩分两排打入地面，填满中间的空隙，做成一道围墙。
There was no gate, because a gate seemed an invitation to whatever might wish to come inside. || 围墙没有门，因为在我看来，门像是在邀请某种东西进来。
I climbed over by a ladder and pulled it after me, leaving no easy way for anyone to follow. || 我用梯子爬进去，再把梯子拉进来，不给任何追随者留下容易进入的通道。
Within the wall I pitched my tent, then began cutting deeper into the rock to make a storehouse. || 在围墙里，我支起帐篷，又开始向岩石深处挖，想做一间储藏室。
The work was slow, and the ground I loosened had to be carried out by the same hands that loosened it. || 干得很慢；挖松的土，还得由这同一双手一点点搬出去。
One day part of the roof fell, and I stood trembling beside the heap where I might have been buried. || 一天，洞顶塌了一部分，我站在本可能把自己埋住的土堆旁，浑身发抖。
After that I set up posts and boards to hold it, learning late what a proper builder would have known before beginning. || 此后我架起柱子和木板支撑洞顶，才学会一个合格的建筑者开工前就该懂的事。
A thunderstorm gave me another terror: one spark might destroy every grain of powder I possessed. || 雷暴又带来一种恐惧：一个火星就可能毁掉我所有的火药。
I divided the powder into small parcels and hid them in separate dry places instead of trusting everything to one barrel. || 我把火药分成小包，分别藏在不同的干燥地方，不再把全部希望寄托于一只桶。
To keep time, I cut marks on a wooden post, making a longer mark for each week and another for each month. || 为了记日子，我在木柱上刻痕，每周刻一道更长的，每月再刻一道特别的。
I began a journal too, using the ink and paper saved from the ship, though neither supply could last forever. || 我也开始写日记，用的是从船上抢救的墨水和纸，尽管两样东西都不可能永远够用。
Writing gave the days a shape, but it could not prevent my thoughts from circling the same miserable questions. || 写作让日子有了轮廓，却阻止不了思绪围着同样的悲苦问题打转。
Why had I alone survived, and what was the use of surviving if I must spend my life speaking to nobody? || 为什么只有我活下来？如果一辈子都无人可说话，活下来又有什么用？
At last I set my condition down in two columns, placing what was bad beside what was good. || 最后，我把处境分成两栏，把坏的一面和好的一面并排写下。
I was alone, certainly, but I was alive; I was far from help, but I had food, shelter, and tools. || 我的确孤身一人，但还活着；我离援助很远，但有食物、住处和工具。
The paper did not change the island, yet it sometimes stopped my grief from pretending that nothing useful remained. || 纸上的字没有改变岛屿，却有时能阻止悲伤让我以为自己已一无所有。
When the ground shook beneath me in an earthquake, I rushed outside and watched pieces of rock tumble down the hill. || 一场地震让脚下的地面摇晃，我冲出去，看着石块从山上滚落。
I had built a little castle against imagined enemies, only to discover that the hill behind it could be dangerous too. || 我建造小城堡来防范想象中的敌人，却发现背后的山也可能带来危险。

# 6. A Voice in the Fever | 高烧中的声音
For a long time I worked as though my health were another tool that could never break. || 很长一段时间，我不停地干活，仿佛健康也是一件永不会坏掉的工具。
Then fever took hold of me, and I lay shivering while the things I needed seemed impossibly far from my bed. || 随后发烧击倒了我，我躺着发抖，所需的东西似乎都离床遥不可及。
There was water nearby, but the thought of rising to fetch it was almost as frightening as the thirst. || 水就在附近，可起身去取水这个念头，几乎和口渴本身一样让人害怕。
I had survived waves and hunger, yet now a few steps across my own room could defeat me. || 我熬过了巨浪和饥饿，如今却可能连在屋里走几步都做不到。
In a troubled sleep I dreamed of a terrible figure descending towards me, and woke with my heart beating wildly. || 不安的睡梦中，我梦见一个可怕的身影向我降来，醒时心跳得厉害。
The dream seemed to accuse me of living without gratitude, despite all the times my life had been spared. || 那个梦仿佛在指责我：生命多次获救，我却仍不知感恩。
I remembered my father's warnings and the promises that had lasted no longer than the storms which produced them. || 我想起父亲的警告，也想起那些只在引发它们的风暴持续时才有效的誓言。
For the first time in many years, I prayed with something more than fear of the next wave. || 多年来头一次，我祈祷时不再只是害怕下一道海浪。
Among the books from the ship was a Bible, and I opened it when my hands were steady enough to hold it. || 从船上带来的书里有一本《圣经》，双手稳得能拿住书时，我把它翻开了。
I had wanted deliverance only from the island; now I began to think that my own conduct needed attention as well. || 我从前只想获救离岛，现在开始觉得，自己的行为也需要反省。
My faith did not make me well in a moment, and several days remained confused and missing from my account. || 信仰并没有让我立刻痊愈，仍有好几天在记忆里混乱不清，没能记下来。
When strength returned, I walked a little at a time, stopping often and learning how weak I had become. || 力气回来后，我每次只走一点，常常停下，才明白自己已经虚弱到什么程度。
The island looked much the same as before, but I could no longer assume I would always be able to work. || 岛屿看起来和以前差不多，可我再也不能认定自己总有力气干活。
Food within reach, dry bedding, and water ready for use became matters to prepare before illness, not during it. || 伸手能取到的食物、干燥的铺盖和备好的水，成了必须在生病前准备、而非病中再操心的事。
I resumed my tasks more slowly, carrying the Bible back to its shelf and the memory of helplessness into my plans. || 我慢慢恢复劳作，把《圣经》放回架上，也把无力自助的记忆带入此后的计划。
There were still mornings when loneliness weighed on me, but it no longer occupied every part of the day. || 仍有一些早晨，孤独沉重地压着我，可它不再占据一天的每个部分。
Work, prayer, and the marks on my wooden calendar gave me ways of answering time instead of merely enduring it. || 劳作、祈祷和木制日历上的刻痕，让我能够回应时间，而不只是忍受它。
I could not say when a ship would come, but I could decide what must be ready before sunset. || 我说不出船什么时候会来，但能决定日落之前必须准备好什么。

# 7. Seeds, Seasons, and Hungry Birds | 种子、季节与饥饿的鸟
Before my illness, I had shaken the dust from an old grain bag onto the ground beside my wall. || 生病之前，我曾把一个旧粮袋里的碎屑抖在围墙旁的地上。
When green shoots appeared there, I looked at them in astonishment, for I had not planted anything. || 那里冒出绿色幼苗时，我惊讶地望着它们，因为自己并没有播种。
Some grew into barley, and others into rice, which seemed almost a miracle until I remembered the bag. || 一些长成了大麦，另一些长成了稻子；在想起那个袋子之前，这几乎像个奇迹。
A few overlooked grains had survived, and rain had done the planting I had never intended. || 几粒遗漏的种子活了下来，雨水替我完成了原本没有打算做的播种。
I watched the small crop carefully, then saved almost every grain instead of eating what might become next year's food. || 我仔细照料这一小片庄稼，随后几乎把所有谷粒都留下，没有吃掉那些可能变成来年粮食的种子。
Yet having seed did not mean that I knew when to plant it, and my first attempt at sowing met the dry season. || 可有种子并不等于知道何时播种，我第一次尝试便碰上了旱季。
The soil hardened, rain failed to come, and I was left staring at ground that offered nothing back. || 土壤变硬，雨迟迟不来，我只能看着那片没有任何回报的地。
Fortunately, I had kept some seed aside, and a later planting showed me what the earlier one had lacked. || 幸运的是，我另留了一些种子；晚些时候的播种让我明白，前一次缺少什么。
Rain, time, and the right season mattered as much as the number of seeds in my hand. || 雨水、时间和合适的季节，与手里的种子数量一样重要。
Goats came to eat the young plants, so I fenced the plot, cutting stakes that would themselves take root. || 山羊来啃嫩苗，于是我围起田地，砍下那些能自行生根的枝条做木桩。
Then birds gathered above it, waiting to take their share as though they had done all the sowing themselves. || 随后鸟儿聚在田地上方，等着分一份粮食，仿佛播种都是它们做的。
For days I guarded the crop, because losing those few heads of grain would cost much more than one meal. || 我守了许多天，因为损失那几穗谷物，代价远不止少吃一顿饭。
Meanwhile I explored farther inland and found a pleasant valley with fruit, clear water, and grass growing thickly. || 与此同时，我向内陆探索得更远，找到一个宜人的山谷，有水果、清水和茂盛的草。
I nearly moved my home there, then remembered that the sea was my only road to the world outside. || 我几乎决定搬过去，随后想起大海是通往外界的唯一道路。
From my old home I could watch for a sail, while the comfortable valley hid the shore from view. || 在旧住处，我还能留意帆影，舒适的山谷却把海岸遮住了。
I kept both places, making a second shelter in the valley and drying grapes to carry back as raisins. || 我保留了两处，在山谷搭建第二个住处，又晒干葡萄，带些葡萄干回来。
Beyond the island I sometimes saw distant land, but I knew nothing certain about the people who might live there. || 我有时能看见岛外远方的陆地，可对那里可能居住的人，一无确切了解。
My eyes had found another shore long before my hands could build any safe means of reaching it. || 我的眼睛早已找到另一片海岸，双手却还造不出能安全到达那里的工具。

# 8. The Long Road to a Loaf | 做出一块面包的漫长道路
As my harvests improved, I began thinking of bread, and soon discovered how much work lay inside that ordinary word. || 随着收成改善，我开始想着面包，很快发现这个普通词语里面藏着多少劳作。
I had grain, but no mill to grind it, no sieve to clean the flour, and no oven in which to bake. || 我有谷物，却没有磨，没有筛面粉的筛子，也没有烤面包的炉子。
Even the baskets needed for storing the crop were missing, though at first I had scarcely thought of them. || 连储存庄稼所需的篮子都没有，只是起初我几乎没想到这一点。
I remembered watching basket-makers at home and tried bending thin branches into the shapes I had seen. || 我想起在家乡看过人编篮子，就试着弯折细枝，编成记忆中的样子。
My first work was crooked, but it held something, and that mattered more than whether a shopkeeper would have bought it. || 最初的作品歪歪扭扭，却能装东西；这比店主愿不愿意买它重要得多。
Pots gave me far more trouble, because the clay cracked, the sides collapsed, or the drying sun left them too weak. || 陶罐给我的麻烦更多：泥会裂，罐壁会塌，或是太阳晒干后仍然不结实。
For a long while I could make vessels to hold dry things, but none that would safely stand upon a fire. || 很长时间里，我能做出盛干东西的容器，却没有一只能安稳放在火上。
Then I noticed a broken piece of clay that had become hard and red after lying among hot coals. || 随后我发现，一块碎陶土在炽热的炭火中待过后，变得坚硬而发红。
I arranged a fire around some of my pots and kept it burning until the clay changed as that fragment had done. || 我围着几个罐子生火，让火持续烧着，直到陶土发生和那块碎片一样的变化。
One vessel survived well enough to hold water over the heat, and I watched it boil with more joy than I can describe. || 一只容器结实得可以装水加热，我看着水沸腾，喜悦难以形容。
For grinding, I hollowed a block of hard wood and made a heavy wooden tool to crush the grain inside it. || 为了磨粮，我挖空一块硬木，又做了沉重的木制工具，用来捣碎里面的谷物。
Old pieces of fine cloth from the sailors' clothes became sieves, though finding suitable scraps took a great deal of searching. || 水手旧衣服上的细布成了筛子，不过找出合适的碎布着实费了一番工夫。
I had no yeast, and making a loaf rise was a difficulty I could not solve, so I baked without it. || 我没有酵母，无法解决让面包膨松的问题，于是只能不放酵母烤制。
I heated a flat hearth with coals, swept it clear, and covered the dough with a broad earthen pot. || 我用炭火烧热平坦的炉台，扫干净后，把面团放上去，再罩一只宽口陶罐。
More coals around the outside kept the heat in, while I waited with the attention I once gave to profitable cargoes. || 在罐外加炭可以保持热度，我专心等待，就像从前盯着能赚钱的货物一样。
The bread was rough, but it was bread made from my own harvest, and I did not find it easy to stop admiring it. || 面包很粗糙，却是用自己种出的粮食做的，我忍不住一遍遍欣赏。
Much of my third year passed among these tasks, with new sowing and harvesting continually interrupting my experiments. || 第三年的大部分时间就在这些活计中度过，新一轮播种和收割不断打断我的试验。
When people buy a loaf, they seldom count the fences, baskets, fires, and seasons that have helped to put it in their hands. || 人买面包时，很少细数那些帮助它来到手里的围栏、篮子、炉火与季节。
I had once been one of those people, and now I counted every one of them. || 我也曾是那样的人，如今却一项一项数得清清楚楚。

# 9. The Boat That Never Sailed | 没有下水的船
From the higher ground I could see distant land, and the sight made my safe little home seem suddenly less satisfying. || 从高处能看到远方的陆地，那景象忽然让安全的小家不再那么令人满足。
I went back to the boat that the storm had thrown ashore, determined to repair it and sail away. || 我返回那只被风暴冲上岸的小艇旁，下定决心修好它，驶离这里。
For weeks I cut levers, brought rollers, and dug away sand, trying to turn the heavy hull upright. || 我花了好几周削杠杆、搬滚木、挖沙子，试着把沉重的艇身翻正。
I tired myself almost beyond belief, yet the boat remained where the sea had chosen to leave it. || 我把自己累得几乎不敢相信，小艇却仍停在大海选定的位置。
Then I decided to make a canoe from a tree trunk, as I had seen people do in other countries. || 随后我决定用树干做独木舟，就像在别的地方见过的那样。
I had better tools than some of those builders, and I thought the task would therefore be easier for me. || 我的工具比一些造舟者更好，因此我以为自己做起来会更容易。
I chose a great tree, spent many days cutting it down, and worked patiently to shape the trunk. || 我选中一棵大树，花许多天砍倒它，又耐心地修整树干。
The inside had to be hollowed, the ends shaped, and the sides made even, all by one pair of hands. || 内部要挖空，两端要修出形状，两侧要弄匀，这一切都只能靠一双手。
Month after month I worked, pleased to see the vessel growing out of the rough wood beneath my tools. || 一个月接一个月，我欣喜地看着工具下的粗木渐渐变成船。
It would hold many men, though I had only one man to put inside it, and I admired its size all the same. || 它大得能装许多人，尽管我只有一个人可以坐进去，我还是欣赏它的体量。
Only when it was finished did I face the question I should have answered before cutting the tree. || 直到造完，我才面对那个砍树之前就该回答的问题。
How was I to get it into the water? || 我该怎样把它弄进水里？
The tree had stood too far from the shore, and the canoe was far too heavy for me to move. || 那棵树离海岸太远，独木舟又重得让我根本搬不动。
I told myself that there must be a way, because accepting failure after so much labor was almost unbearable. || 我告诉自己总会有办法，因为付出这么多劳动后承认失败，实在难以忍受。
I considered digging a channel and worked out how long such a ditch would take a solitary man to make. || 我考虑挖一条水道，计算一个孤身的人挖出这样的沟要多久。
The answer was so discouraging that even my stubborn hope could find no useful reply. || 答案令人灰心到了极点，连我固执的希望也找不出有用的回应。
The great canoe stayed beneath the trees, neither a wreck nor a vessel, but the result of a question asked too late. || 大独木舟留在树下，既不是残骸，也不算能用的船，而是一个问得太迟的问题留下的结果。
I returned to my home, where there was planting to be done regardless of how disappointed I felt. || 我回到住处，无论多么失望，都还有地要种。
My axe had done what I commanded, and the tree had given me what I wanted; the mistake had been entirely mine. || 斧头照我的命令干了活，树也给出了我想要的东西，错误全在我自己。

# 10. A Smaller Boat and a Greater Sea | 小船与更大的海
Years passed, and I began to reckon my possessions less by their number than by how long they would last. || 年复一年，我衡量物资时渐渐不再只看数量，而是看它们还能用多久。
My clothes wore thin, my ink ran out, and every shot reminded me that no new barrel of powder would arrive. || 衣服磨薄了，墨水用尽了，每一次开枪都提醒我，不会有新火药送来。
I made clothes from goatskins and learned to catch goats alive instead of depending forever on hunting them. || 我用山羊皮做衣服，又学着活捉山羊，不再永远依赖猎杀。
When a trapped goat struggled against me, I thought more carefully about what would happen if my guns became useless. || 一只落入陷阱的山羊拼命挣扎时，我更认真地想到，如果枪不能用了会怎样。
I fenced a place for the animals, brought them food, and gradually made a herd that could supply milk as well as meat. || 我围出一块地方养它们，给它们喂食，渐渐有了一群既能供奶又能供肉的山羊。
The ship's dog, the cats, and a parrot I called Poll formed the only household I could claim. || 船上的狗、猫和一只被我叫作波尔的鹦鹉，组成了我唯一能称作家人的一群。
I taught Poll words by repeating them during work, until at last another voice began to break the island's silence. || 干活时，我反复说话教波尔，直到终于有另一个声音打破岛上的寂静。
Meanwhile I made a smaller canoe, taking care this time to choose a place from which I could actually launch it. || 与此同时，我做了更小的独木舟，这一次谨慎地选了一个真能把它送入水中的位置。
It could carry me along the coast, but I would not trust it for the long crossing to the distant land. || 它能载我沿岸划行，可我不敢靠它远航去那片陆地。
One day I set out to explore the island by water, carrying food and thinking myself a fairly capable boatman. || 一天，我带着食物出发，想从水上探索全岛，自以为划船已有几分本领。
Beyond a rocky point, the current seized the canoe and carried it away from shore faster than I could paddle. || 绕过一处岩石岬角后，水流攫住小舟，以我划桨赶不上的速度，把它带离海岸。
I strained until my arms ached, watching the island grow smaller while all my comfortable arrangements remained upon it. || 我划得双臂酸痛，看着岛屿越来越小，而辛苦安置好的一切都留在岛上。
The home I had wished to escape now seemed the best place on earth, if only I could reach it again. || 那个曾想逃离的家，如今仿佛成了世上最好的地方，只要我还能回得去。
I thought of my full storehouse, my goats, and my dry bed with a longing greater than any desire for distant riches. || 我想着满满的储藏室、山羊和干燥的床，渴望之强烈，超过了对任何远方财富的向往。
At last a change in the wind helped me towards another current, which ran nearer the island instead of away from it. || 终于，风向变化帮我靠近另一股水流，它朝岛边流去，而不是远离岛屿。
I guided the canoe into that water and reached a safe cove, too tired to rejoice properly. || 我把小舟引入那股水流，抵达安全的小湾，却累得连欢喜都表达不出来。
Leaving the boat there, I crossed the island on foot and rested at my shelter in the valley. || 我把船留在那里，步行穿过岛屿，在山谷的住处休息。
A voice called my name while I slept, and I started up, certain for a moment that someone had found me. || 睡梦中，有个声音呼唤我的名字，我惊醒了，一瞬间确信有人找到了我。
It was Poll, repeating words I had taught him, and I was glad of the bird while wishing he were a man. || 原来是波尔在重复我教过的话；我很高兴它在，又希望它能是个人。
For a long time afterward, I was content to watch the sea without trying to make it carry me anywhere. || 此后很长时间，我满足于望着大海，不再试图让它把我载去别处。

# 11. One Footprint | 一个脚印
By my fifteenth year on the island, I had become accustomed to paths on which no stranger ever walked. || 到了在岛上的第十五年，我已习惯那些从没有陌生人经过的小路。
Then, one day upon the shore, I saw the print of a naked human foot in the sand. || 随后有一天，在岸边，我看见了沙地上一个赤裸人脚的印痕。
I stopped as if something had struck me, looking first behind me and then along the empty beach. || 我像被什么击中了一样停下，先看身后，再看空荡荡的海滩。
There was no boat, no person, and no second print to explain the first. || 没有船，没有人，也没有第二个脚印能解释第一个。
I examined the toes and heel, hoping that a mark so plain might somehow prove to be only my imagination. || 我仔细查看脚趾与脚跟的印痕，盼望如此清晰的痕迹竟然只是自己的想象。
Then I fled towards home, seeing possible men behind every bush and hearing danger in every sound. || 随后我逃回家，把每丛灌木后面都想成可能藏着人，把每个声音都听成危险。
I could hardly remember how I got over my wall, or whether I had used the ladder at all. || 我几乎记不起怎样翻过围墙，甚至不记得是否用了梯子。
For days I stayed inside, though milk must be taken from the goats and food must still be gathered. || 我躲在里面好几天，可山羊仍要挤奶，食物也仍要采集。
I had prayed for human company, yet the first sign of it had made the whole island frightening. || 我曾祈求有人相伴，可第一个人的迹象，却使整座岛变得可怕。
Could the print have been my own, left during an earlier walk and preserved by some chance? || 脚印会不会是我以前散步时自己留下的，偶然保存到了现在？
That thought quieted me enough to return, but my foot did not fit the mark, which was larger. || 这个念头让我平静得敢于返回查看，可我的脚与印痕不符，那个脚印更大。
I searched the nearby ground, finding neither a clear answer nor enough evidence to put my fear to rest. || 我搜索附近地面，既没有找到明确答案，也没有找到足以让恐惧平息的证据。
At moments I imagined the Devil had made it, then argued with myself that such a trick made very little sense. || 有时我想象是魔鬼留下的，又劝自己说这样的把戏实在没有多少道理。
The more ordinary explanation, that a visitor had landed, seemed almost as difficult to bear. || 更普通的解释——有来访者登岸——却几乎同样难以承受。
I strengthened my wall, planted thick cover outside it, and took greater care that my movements could not be seen. || 我加固围墙，在外面种上浓密的遮挡物，更小心地不让自己的活动被发现。
My goats were divided between different places, so that losing one herd would not mean losing everything. || 我把山羊分养在不同地方，免得失去一群就等于失去全部。
These precautions took months, and the fear behind them lasted much longer than the work itself. || 这些防范措施费了几个月，推动它们的恐惧却比劳作持续得更久。
On a clear morning the island could look exactly as it had before, until I remembered that somebody else's foot had touched it. || 晴朗的早晨，岛屿看起来和从前完全一样，直到我记起另一只脚曾踏上它。
I had learned to provide for almost every bodily need, but I had found no storehouse in which to lock away a thought. || 我已学会满足几乎每一种身体需求，却找不到一间能把念头锁起来的储藏室。

# 12. Fires on the Far Shore | 远处海岸的火光
On a remote part of the coast, I later found human bones and signs of fires beside a place where canoes might land. || 后来，在偏远海岸的一处，我发现人骨和烧火的痕迹，旁边适合独木舟靠岸。
The sight filled me with horror, and I returned without examining more than I could bear. || 那景象让我惊骇万分，我只看了自己还能承受的一点，就返回了。
I believed visitors were bringing prisoners there to kill and eat them, though I knew nothing of their names or homes. || 我认为来访者把俘虏带到这里杀死并吃掉，可对他们的名字和家园一无所知。
For a long time I imagined attacking them from hiding, and arranged whole battles in which I was always the victor. || 很长时间里，我想象从暗处攻击他们，在脑中安排整场战斗，而胜者总是自己。
Then I considered how many men might come after those I killed, and how little my imagined victories resembled a safe plan. || 随后我想到，杀死一些人后还会引来多少人，脑中的胜利与安全的计划又相差多远。
Another question troubled me too: what authority had I to appoint myself judge over people who had never harmed me? || 另一个问题也困扰着我：这些人从未伤害过我，我有什么权力自封为审判者？
I did not approve of what I thought they did, but certainty about my own beliefs did not settle every question. || 我不赞成自己认定他们做的事，可确信自己的信念，并不能解决所有问题。
I gave up the plan of a secret massacre and resolved to keep out of sight unless my own safety required action. || 我放弃秘密屠杀的计划，决定保持隐蔽，除非自身安全要求我采取行动。
A hidden cave offered another shelter, where I stored supplies and powder away from my main home. || 一个隐蔽山洞提供了另一处藏身地，我把物资和火药存进去，远离主要住处。
Even cooking needed care, because a line of smoke might tell someone exactly where to look for me. || 连做饭也要小心，因为一缕烟就可能告诉别人该到哪里找我。
Years later, during a stormy night, I heard guns at sea and climbed up with a light to signal the shore. || 多年后，一个风暴夜里，我听见海上传来炮声，便带着灯爬上高处，示意这里有岸。
In the morning a wreck lay upon distant rocks, and I watched it with a hope I scarcely dared to name. || 早晨，一艘失事的船躺在远处礁石上，我带着几乎不敢说出口的希望望着它。
Surely, I thought, one living man might have escaped, and one voice might at last answer mine. || 我想，总该有一个活人逃出来吧，总该终于有一个声音能回答我吧。
I went out to the wreck when I judged the water safe enough, but found no survivor aboard. || 当我判断水面足够安全时，便划去失事的船旁，却没在船上找到幸存者。
There were useful things to bring away, including food, clothes, and money, but the company I wanted was absent. || 有食物、衣服和钱等有用的东西可以搬走，可我渴望的同伴并不存在。
Two men lay dead inside, and the silence around them was harder to endure than any empty room at home. || 船内躺着两个死去的人，他们周围的寂静，比家里任何空房间都更难忍受。
I returned with another load of possessions and the old loneliness made newly painful by disappointed hope. || 我带着又一批物资回来，而旧有的孤独因希望落空再次变得疼痛。
At night I dreamed of a prisoner running from his captors towards me, and of saving him before they reached us. || 夜里，我梦见一个俘虏挣脱抓捕者，向我跑来，我在他们赶到前救下了他。
The dream faded after waking, but the wish for a companion remained with me when I walked the shore. || 梦在醒后消散，可沿岸行走时，想要同伴的愿望一直伴着我。

# 13. The Man I Called Friday | 我称为星期五的人
One morning I saw several canoes on the shore, and through my glass watched a gathering beside their fires. || 一天早晨，我看见岸边有几只独木舟，用望远镜观察聚在火边的人。
There were prisoners among them, and suddenly one broke away and ran with a speed that gave his pursuers little advantage. || 其中有俘虏，忽然一个人挣脱出来飞奔，速度快得追赶者难以赶上。
He came towards my side of the island, and I stood watching a real danger take the shape of my old dream. || 他向我所在的岛边跑来，我看着真实危险渐渐变成旧日梦中的样子。
When he crossed a stream by swimming, one pursuer stopped, while two others continued after him. || 他游过一条溪流时，一个追赶者停下了，另两个继续追来。
I ran out with my guns, placing myself between the exhausted man and those who meant to capture him again. || 我拿着枪跑出去，站在筋疲力尽的人与那些想再次抓住他的人之间。
I struck one pursuer down, and when the other raised his bow, I fired before his arrow could reach me. || 我击倒一个追赶者，另一个举弓时，我在他的箭射到之前开了枪。
The first man began to recover, but the fugitive took my sword and killed him before he could rise. || 第一个人开始苏醒，可逃亡者拿了我的剑，在他起身前杀死了他。
The rescued man then knelt, bent his head to the ground, and placed my foot upon it. || 获救的人随后跪下，把头伏在地上，又把我的脚放到头上。
I understood this as a promise to serve me, and led him away before the others could discover what had happened. || 我把这理解为服侍我的承诺，趁其他人发现之前带他离开。
I gave him food, water, and a place to sleep, watching his tired face as he slowly understood that he was safe. || 我给他食物、水和睡觉的地方，看着他疲惫的脸慢慢明白自己安全了。
Because I had saved him on a Friday, I called him Friday; it was a name I gave, not one I learned from him. || 因为是在星期五救的他，我便叫他星期五；这是我给的名字，不是从他那里问来的。
The first English words I taught him included yes, no, and master, by which I wished him to address me. || 最初教他的英语包括“是”“不”和“主人”，我希望他用最后一个词称呼我。
We began with gestures and objects, repeating names until a cup, a loaf, or a tool could be asked for without pointing. || 我们先靠手势和实物，反复练名字，直到要杯子、面包或工具时不必再用手指。
My ordinary meals were strange to him, and my gun seemed stranger still, until I showed him how it worked. || 我的日常食物对他很陌生，枪更陌生，直到我向他展示它的用法。
He had his own knowledge of boats, water, and the nearby country, which I could not have learned by remaining alone. || 他懂船、水和附近的地方，这些知识是我独自生活时不可能学到的。
I enlarged our supply of grain and made room for another person's needs, finding new pleasure in old tasks. || 我增加粮食储备，为另一个人的需要作安排，从旧活计中发现了新的乐趣。
When I spoke, someone listened; when work was difficult, another pair of hands was there beside mine. || 我说话时有人听着，活计艰难时，自己的双手旁还有另一双手。
I had imagined a helper often enough, but the sound of a man moving about my home was greater comfort than I had expected. || 我不止一次想象有个帮手，可一个人在家中活动的声音，带来的安慰仍超过预期。
The island had not moved an inch nearer England, yet my life upon it had changed beyond calculation. || 岛屿丝毫没有向英国靠近，可我在上面的生活，变化大得难以计算。

# 14. What Friday Knew | 星期五知道的事
As Friday's English improved, I told him about my country and questioned him closely about his own. || 随着星期五的英语进步，我告诉他自己的国家，也仔细询问他的家乡。
He explained that the people he knew ate prisoners taken in war, not every stranger who came among them. || 他解释说，他所知的人会吃战争中抓到的俘虏，并不是把每一个外来者都吃掉。
He also told me that men from a wreck had been welcomed and were still living on the nearby mainland. || 他还告诉我，一些遭遇海难的人受到接纳，至今仍住在附近大陆上。
This news opened a possibility I had never imagined, for I had pictured that coast as nothing but danger. || 这消息打开了我从未想到的可能，因为我一直把那片海岸想成只有危险。
I taught him my religion too, believing that I was giving him knowledge of the greatest importance. || 我也教他自己的宗教，相信自己传授的是最重要的知识。
Sometimes his questions were so direct that I found my own answers much less ready than I had expected. || 有时他的问题直截了当，让我发现自己的回答远没有想象中那么现成。
If God was stronger than the Devil, he asked, why did God not destroy the Devil and put an end to evil? || 他问，如果上帝比魔鬼强大，为什么不消灭魔鬼，把邪恶终结？
I paused over that question, discovering that teaching a belief was not always the same as explaining every part of it. || 我被这个问题问住了，发现教人一种信仰，并不总等于能解释其中每一个部分。
One clear day Friday saw his homeland across the water and sprang up with joy, pointing and calling to me. || 一个晴朗的日子，星期五看到了海那边的家乡，欣喜地跳起来，指着那边呼唤我。
His happiness disturbed me, because I began imagining that he wished to leave me and might return with enemies. || 他的快乐却让我不安，我开始想象他想离开，还可能带着敌人回来。
For weeks I watched him more closely, letting my suspicion turn an innocent joy into something threatening. || 几周间，我更紧地观察他，让自己的猜疑把纯真的快乐变成了威胁。
I was wrong, and afterward I was ashamed of treating his affection for his own country as evidence against him. || 我错了，后来也为把他对家乡的感情当作可疑证据而羞愧。
When I offered him a boat to return in, he made it plain that he did not wish to go without me. || 我提出给他一只船回去时，他明确表示，不愿丢下我独自离开。
Together we built a canoe large enough for a longer voyage, and his skill with it was much better than mine. || 我们一起造了一只足以远航的独木舟，而他操舟的本领比我强得多。
Before we sailed, more visitors came with prisoners, one of whom I recognized through the glass as a European. || 出航前，又有来访者带着俘虏到来，我从望远镜里认出其中一人是欧洲人。
Friday and I attacked to rescue them, and after the fighting we found an older prisoner lying bound inside a canoe. || 我和星期五发动攻击救人，战斗后，在一只独木舟里发现一个被绑着的年长俘虏。
Friday looked at him, cried out, and threw himself beside him with a joy that needed no English to explain. || 星期五一看见他便喊起来，扑到身边，那种喜悦无需英语解释。
The old man was his father, and Friday kissed him, held him, and fetched food and water with tireless care. || 老人是他的父亲；星期五亲吻他、抱住他，又不知疲倦地取来食物和水照料他。
The other rescued man was Spanish, and told us of more shipwrecked companions still waiting on the mainland. || 另一个获救者是西班牙人，他告诉我们，还有更多遭遇海难的同伴等在大陆上。
We planned to bring them over, but first planted more grain, because a promise of shelter would not feed so many people. || 我们计划把他们接过来，但先多种粮，因为答应提供住处，并不能让这么多人吃饱。
Only when food was ready did Friday's father and the Spaniard leave in a canoe to fetch the others. || 等食物准备好，星期五的父亲和西班牙人才乘独木舟去接其他人。

# 15. The Captain Without a Ship | 没有船的船长
While we waited for their return, Friday came running to tell me that a boat was approaching from the sea. || 等待他们返回时，星期五跑来告诉我，海上有一只小艇驶近。
Beyond it stood an English ship, a sight that should have filled me with nothing but happiness. || 小艇后面停着一艘英国船，那景象本该只让我充满快乐。
Yet the place where she had stopped was unusual, and I watched before showing myself to anyone. || 可它停靠的位置不寻常，我先观察，没有向任何人暴露自己。
The boat landed with three prisoners, and the sailors left them upon the sand while they wandered inland. || 小艇载着三个俘虏靠岸，水手们把他们留在沙地上，自己向内陆走去。
When the guards were out of hearing, I approached the prisoners and asked what had brought them to such a condition. || 等看守走到听不见的地方，我靠近俘虏，问是什么使他们落到这种处境。
One was the ship's captain; his crew had mutinied and meant to leave him and his companions on the island. || 其中一人是船长，船员发动了叛变，打算把他和同伴丢在岛上。
He stared at my strange clothes and weapons, uncertain whether I was a real man or an answer to his prayers. || 他盯着我奇怪的衣服和武器，不确定我是一个真人，还是祈祷得来的回应。
I promised help on two conditions: he must respect my authority here, and take Friday and me to England if he recovered the ship. || 我答应帮助，但有两个条件：在这里听从我的指挥，夺回船后带我和星期五去英国。
He agreed eagerly, and we examined the problem instead of letting hope send us straight into danger. || 他急切地答应，我们仔细分析问题，没有任凭希望把我们直接推入危险。
Some sailors had led the mutiny, he said, while others had joined through fear and might yet change sides. || 他说，有些水手是叛变首领，另一些因恐惧而加入，或许还能转回我们这边。
We freed the prisoners, armed them, and surprised the first party while its men were divided and unprepared. || 我们解开俘虏、给他们武器，趁第一批人分散且毫无准备时突然动手。
The captain persuaded several to obey him again, and we separated those whose promises seemed least reliable. || 船长说服几人重新服从，把那些承诺最不可信的人分开关押。
Then the ship sent a second boat to discover why the first had not returned, bringing more armed men than we wished to face. || 随后船上派出第二只艇，查问第一只为何未归，来的人带着武器，人数多得让我们不愿正面交手。
They shouted, searched the shore, and finally began going back when no voice answered them. || 他们呼喊、搜索海岸，没有声音回应，最后准备返回。
If they reached the ship with news of danger, the crew might sail away and leave our chance behind with us. || 如果他们带着危险的消息回到船上，船员可能立即开船，把我们的机会也抛下。
I sent Friday and another man through the woods to call out, keeping well hidden while drawing the searchers farther inland. || 我派星期五和另一个人穿过树林呼喊，让他们藏好身形，把搜索者引向更深的内陆。
Every time the sailors followed a voice, it came again from farther away, until their legs were tired and darkness surrounded them. || 水手们每追向一个声音，声音又从更远处传来，直到腿脚疲惫，四周陷入黑暗。
We took their boat while they searched, then confronted them at night as if a powerful force controlled the island. || 他们搜索时，我们夺下小艇，夜里再出面，装出一支强大力量控制全岛的样子。
Most surrendered, and the captain could at last choose enough loyal men to attempt the greater prize. || 多数人投降了，船长终于能挑出足够忠诚的人，去争取更大的目标。
He left with them to board his ship, while I remained ashore listening to a darkness that could tell me nothing. || 他带人去登自己的船，我留在岸上听着黑夜，可黑夜无法告诉我任何消息。
At last the agreed signal sounded from the sea, and I knew that the vessel was his again. || 终于，海上传来了约定的信号，我知道船重新归他了。
The next morning he came to me smiling, with clothes from the ship and news of the passage I had waited for so long. || 第二天早晨，他微笑着来到我面前，带来船上的衣服，以及我等了那么久的返乡航程的消息。

# 16. The Things I Carried Away | 我带走的东西
When the captain told me I was free to leave, I could not answer as promptly as I had answered ordinary questions. || 船长说我可以离开时，我没能像回答普通问题那样立刻作答。
I held him, wept, and thanked God, feeling that the years upon the island had all arrived together in that single moment. || 我抱住他，流泪感谢上帝，觉得岛上的岁月全在这一刻同时涌来。
New clothes were welcome, though my body had become so used to goatskins that ordinary garments felt strange against it. || 新衣服自然受欢迎，可身体已经穿惯了山羊皮，普通衣物贴上来反而陌生。
Some mutineers were to remain behind, and I explained the island's stores, fences, crops, and goats before I left. || 有些叛变者将留下来，我离开前向他们说明岛上的物资、围栏、庄稼与山羊。
The captain added supplies, and I gave directions about growing food rather than leaving them with nothing but punishment. || 船长补给了物资，我则教他们怎样种粮，并不是只把惩罚留给他们。
I also left a message for the Spaniard and Friday's father, who had not yet returned with their companions. || 我还给尚未带同伴返回的西班牙人和星期五的父亲留下了消息。
Friday came with me, and among my possessions I chose my goatskin cap, my umbrella, and one of my parrots. || 星期五与我同行，我从物品中选了山羊皮帽、伞和一只鹦鹉带走。
The money I had once laughed at went aboard as well, its silver darkened by years of having nothing to buy. || 我曾嘲笑的钱币也上了船，那些银币因多年无处可用而发黑。
I had spent twenty-eight years on the island, building a life that I now had to leave in other people's hands. || 我在岛上度过了二十八年，建成的生活，如今不得不交到别人手里。
From the ship I looked towards the shore, where no wall or planted field could be seen clearly enough to distinguish my work. || 我从船上望向岸边，看不清围墙或种过的田，无法从景色里辨出自己的劳动。
The island seemed smaller at a distance, though nothing I remembered from it had become small. || 远处的岛屿显得小了，可记忆中的一切并没有变小。
When I reached England, my parents were dead, and many people had long believed that I was dead too. || 我抵达英国时，父母已经去世，许多人也早就以为我死了。
I could come home at last, but I could not bring home the years in which they had waited for news. || 我终于可以回家，却带不回他们等待消息的那些年。
In Lisbon I found the Portuguese captain who had once rescued Xury and me, now grown old but still my faithful friend. || 在里斯本，我找到当年救过我和佐立的葡萄牙船长；他已年老，仍是我忠实的朋友。
He helped me recover the income from my Brazilian plantation, which had prospered while I knew nothing of it. || 他帮我取回巴西种植园的收益；在我全不知情的岁月里，种植园一直兴旺。
Papers and accounts showed me that I was wealthy, and for a while I scarcely knew how to receive the news. || 文件和账目显示我已很富有，一时间，我几乎不知道该怎样接受这个消息。
I provided for those who had helped me, while discovering that money had regained its power now that I was among people again. || 我照顾那些帮助过自己的人，也发现重回人群以后，金钱又恢复了力量。
Afraid of another long sea journey, I returned towards England by land, traveling with companions through mountains and winter weather. || 害怕再经历漫长海路，我选择从陆路返回英国，与同伴穿过山地和冬日风雪。
That journey brought dangers of its own, and Friday helped protect us when wolves attacked along the difficult roads. || 那段路也有自身的危险，狼在难走的山路上袭击时，星期五帮助保护了我们。
At last I settled, married, and had children, finding the quiet life my father had urged upon me so long before. || 最后，我定居、结婚、生了孩子，过上父亲很久以前劝我选择的平静生活。
Yet the wish to travel did not leave me entirely, even after I had learned so much of what it could cost. || 可即使已经深知出行可能付出怎样的代价，旅行的愿望仍没完全离开我。
After my wife died, I sailed again and visited the island, where others were now working the ground I had once worked alone. || 妻子去世后，我又一次出海，重访那座岛；我曾独自耕作的土地，如今有别人在劳作。
I looked upon it as my own place still, and made arrangements for the people living there before continuing my voyage. || 我仍把它看作自己的地方，替住在那里的人作了安排，然后继续航行。
Thus I could reckon the years, the acres, and the money well enough, but I could not reckon all the changes in myself. || 岁月、土地和钱财，我都算得清，可发生在自身的种种变化，却算不清。
Whenever I remembered the island, I saw not only the shore where I had nearly died, but the daily work by which I had lived. || 每当想起那座岛，我看到的不只是险些丧命的海岸，还有使我活下来的每日劳作。
