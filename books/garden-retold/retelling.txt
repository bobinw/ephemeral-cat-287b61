A Lonely Child in a Large House
Mary Lennox grew up with money but little affection.
After disease took her parents, she was sent from India to her uncle's house in England.
Mr. Craven had lost his wife and spent much of his time away.
His large house felt full of closed doors and secrets.
Mary expected servants to do everything for her.
A young maid, Martha, spoke plainly and encouraged her to go outside.
At first, Mary disliked the cold air and unfamiliar people.
Then she began to notice things beyond her own unhappiness.

The Door No One Opened
Mary heard about a garden that had been locked for ten years.
Her uncle's wife had loved it before she died.
Grief had made him close its door and bury the key.
A robin became Mary's small companion outdoors.
Near it, she found the old key in the earth.
Later, the wind moved some hanging branches and showed her a hidden door.
Mary unlocked it and stepped into the forgotten garden.
It looked asleep, but small green shoots showed that life remained.

Growing Things Together
Mary began clearing space around the shoots.
Work gave her an appetite, and the air helped her sleep.
She met Martha's brother, Dickon, who understood plants and animals.
He could be gentle without making a show of it.
Mary trusted him with the secret of the garden.
They planted seeds and watched for new leaves.
Mary learned that care meant doing small things again and again.
As she helped the garden grow, her own life became less lonely.

The Boy Behind Another Door
One night, Mary followed the sound of crying through the house.
She found her cousin Colin, who spent nearly all his time in bed.
He feared that he would become disabled and die young.
The adults' fear and whispers had made his fear grow.
Colin ordered people around, and Mary recognized something of herself in him.
She refused to obey every angry demand.
But she also listened and told him about the garden.
For the first time, Colin wanted something beyond his sickroom.

Learning to Stand
Mary and Dickon brought Colin into the garden in his wheelchair.
There, he saw living things that did not spend all day discussing his illness.
The gardener, Ben Weatherstaff, discovered the children and recognized Colin.
When Ben spoke about his weak legs, Colin tried standing to prove him wrong.
He stood with help, then began practicing carefully.
Food, rest, movement, and friendship gradually made him stronger.
He called the wonderful changes magic, though the work happened a little at a time.
His friends helped him hope without expecting every change to happen in a day.

The Garden Opens Again
Far away, Mr. Craven began to feel that he should return home.
A dream of his wife and a letter helped bring him back.
He walked toward the garden he had avoided for so long.
Suddenly, Colin came running out of its door.
Father and son met in astonishment and joy.
Colin showed him the growing garden and told him about the friends who had helped.
The garden had not erased their loss, but it had made room for life beside it.
Together, they walked back to the house with a future they could begin to share.
