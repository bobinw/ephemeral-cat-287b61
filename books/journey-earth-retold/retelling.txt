# 地心游记 · 原作探险版
# 每行：英文｜对应中文；每节为一章。
## The Paper in the Book|旧书里的纸条
My name is Axel, and I lived in Hamburg with my uncle, Professor Lidenbrock.|我叫阿克塞尔，和叔叔利登布洛克教授一起住在汉堡。
He studied rocks, but his temper was often more explosive than any volcano.|他研究岩石，可他的脾气往往比火山还容易爆发。
One afternoon, he brought home an old Icelandic book and opened it with shining eyes.|一天下午，他带回一本冰岛古书，双眼发亮地翻开它。
A small piece of parchment slipped between the pages and fell to the floor.|一小片羊皮纸从书页间滑出，落在地板上。
Strange letters covered it, and the name Arne Saknussemm appeared in the book.|纸上写满奇怪的字母，书里还出现了阿尔内·萨克努塞姆的名字。
My uncle knew the name: this man had been an Icelandic scholar long before our time.|叔叔知道这个名字：那是一位生活在很久以前的冰岛学者。
He changed the strange signs into familiar letters, but the words still made no sense.|他把奇怪的符号换成熟悉的字母，可那些词仍然毫无意义。
Dinner waited while he moved letters across his desk; a mystery mattered more than food.|晚饭摆在那里，他却仍在桌前摆弄字母；谜题比吃饭更重要。
I watched him search for a secret, never imagining that the secret would lead us underground.|我看着他寻找秘密，完全没想到这个秘密会把我们引向地下。

## Reading Backwards|倒过来读
My uncle tried one arrangement after another, and none of them worked.|叔叔试了一种又一种排列方式，没有一种行得通。
Later, while he was out, I lifted the paper and fanned myself with it.|后来，他出门时，我拿起那张纸，给自己扇风。
As the letters moved before my eyes, a few familiar Latin words suddenly appeared.|字母在眼前晃动时，几个熟悉的拉丁词忽然出现了。
The message could be read backwards; I had found the key by accident.|这段文字可以倒过来读；我意外地找到了钥匙。
It described a path inside an Icelandic volcano called Sneffels, now usually called Snaefellsjokull.|它描述了一条通往冰岛斯奈费尔火山内部的道路，这座火山如今通常叫斯奈山冰川火山。
Before the end of June, the shadow of a nearby peak would point to the right opening.|六月结束前，附近一座山峰的影子会指出正确的入口。
The writer claimed that a bold traveller could follow this road towards the Earth's centre.|写信人声称，勇敢的旅行者可以沿着这条路，走向地球中心。
I thought of heat, darkness, and falling rocks, and almost destroyed the message.|我想到高温、黑暗和落石，差一点就毁掉了这张纸。
But I finally told my uncle, and his joy was exactly what I had feared.|可我最后还是告诉了叔叔，而他的狂喜，恰恰就是我所害怕的。
He began planning the journey at once; for him, the words were an invitation.|他立刻开始安排旅行；在他眼里，这些文字就是一封邀请函。

## North to Iceland|向北去冰岛
I argued that the Earth must grow hotter as we went deeper, until no person could survive.|我争辩说，越往地下走，温度一定越高，直到人根本无法生存。
My uncle answered with theories of his own and refused to let my objections stop him.|叔叔用自己的理论回答，不肯让我的反对阻止他。
I loved my home, and I was especially sorry to leave Grauben, who lived with us.|我舍不得家，更舍不得和我们住在一起的格劳本。
She encouraged me to travel, although I had hoped she would persuade me to stay.|她却鼓励我去旅行，虽然我本希望她劝我留下。
We crossed Denmark, sailed north, and at last reached Iceland, where lava shaped the land.|我们穿过丹麦，向北航行，终于抵达冰岛，那里的大地有着熔岩塑造的模样。
In Reykjavik we hired Hans, a quiet Icelandic hunter who knew how to travel through rough country.|在雷克雅未克，我们雇用了汉斯；他是一位沉默的冰岛猎人，擅长穿行崎岖的荒野。
Hans said little, examined our equipment, and carried out each task without hurry.|汉斯话很少，仔细检查装备，不慌不忙地完成每一件事。
We packed food, ropes, lamps, instruments, and tools for breaking stone.|我们装好食物、绳索、灯具、仪器，还有凿开岩石的工具。
As we rode towards the volcano, the bright world around me already seemed precious.|骑马前往火山时，我已经觉得周围明亮的世界无比珍贵。

## The Shadow's Door|影子指向的门
The climb was hard, and loose stones slid away beneath our feet.|攀登十分艰难，松动的石块不断从脚下滑走。
At the top, three dark openings waited inside the great crater.|山顶的巨大火山口内，有三个黑暗的洞口。
Which one had Saknussemm entered? The message depended on a shadow, but clouds hid the sun.|萨克努塞姆进了哪一个？纸条要靠影子指路，可云层遮住了太阳。
My uncle watched the sky impatiently; I secretly hoped the clouds would remain.|叔叔焦躁地盯着天空；我却暗暗希望云一直不散。
At last sunlight broke through, and the mountain's shadow marked one of the openings.|终于，阳光穿透云层，山峰的影子标出了其中一个洞口。
We lowered our supplies on ropes and began descending after them.|我们用绳子放下物资，随后也开始往下攀。
The daylight above shrank into a small circle, while every movement sent echoes down the shaft.|头顶的日光缩成一个小圆，每一个动作都在深井里激起回声。
Hans tested the way before us; my uncle studied the rock as if he were reading a book.|汉斯在前面试探道路；叔叔研究岩石，就像在读一本书。
I held tightly to the rope and tried not to imagine how far I might fall.|我紧紧抓住绳索，努力不去想自己可能会坠落多深。
We had left the ordinary world, and every step now made the return longer.|我们离开了日常的世界，而现在每走一步，归途就会更长。

## A Road Without Water|没有水的道路
Far below the crater, a passage divided, and my uncle chose a road leading east.|在火山口深处，一条通道分岔了，叔叔选择向东的那条路。
For days we walked through layers of rock that told stories of the ancient Earth.|好几天里，我们穿过一层层岩石，它们诉说着远古地球的故事。
Some held traces of plants from forests that had vanished long before people existed.|有些岩层留着植物的痕迹，来自人类出现之前早已消失的森林。
My uncle was delighted by these discoveries; I watched our water supply grow smaller.|叔叔为这些发现兴奋不已；我却看着饮水一点点减少。
At last a wall blocked the passage, and the road had nowhere left to go.|终于，一面石壁挡住通道，这条路到头了。
We had to turn back, spending strength and water simply to reach the fork again.|我们不得不折返，消耗力气和饮水，仅仅为了回到岔路口。
My mouth felt dry, my legs grew weak, and even speaking became difficult.|我的嘴干得厉害，双腿越来越软，连说话都变得困难。
My uncle shared the last of his water with me, but could not make more appear.|叔叔把最后的水分给我，却没办法变出更多水来。
He asked for one more attempt along the other passage, while I wondered whether I could stand.|他请求沿另一条通道再试一次，我却怀疑自己还能不能站起来。
The promise of discovery meant little now beside the thought of one cool drink.|此刻，发现奇迹的希望，远不如喝上一口凉水那么重要。

## Hans Hears a Stream|汉斯听见了水声
Hans listened to the wall, then brought his ear close to the stone.|汉斯侧耳听着岩壁，随后把耳朵贴近石头。
Somewhere behind it, water was moving; he began striking the rock with his pick.|岩壁后面的某个地方，水正在流动；他开始用镐凿石头。
We watched the hole deepen until a sudden jet burst into the passage.|我们看着凿口越来越深，直到一股水突然喷进通道。
It was hot, and Hans pulled back from the spray before we let it cool.|水很烫，汉斯急忙躲开喷溅的水花，我们等着它冷却。
Never had water tasted so good, and I felt life returning with each swallow.|我从没觉得水这么好喝，每咽下一口，都感到生命正在回来。
The stream ran downhill along our route, becoming a companion we could hear in the darkness.|溪水顺着我们的路线向下流去，成了黑暗中一个听得见的同伴。
My uncle named it the Hansbach, in honour of the man who had found it.|叔叔把它命名为汉斯溪，纪念找到水的人。
With water beside us, I began noticing the strange beauty of the shining rock again.|有水相伴，我又开始注意到岩石闪光的奇异美丽。
My uncle checked his instruments and argued that the temperature did not rise as much as expected.|叔叔查看仪器，认为温度并没有像预想的那样升高。
I still doubted his theories, but now I had enough strength to ask questions.|我仍然怀疑他的理论，可现在，我至少有力气提问了。

## Alone in the Dark|独自在黑暗里
One day I walked ahead, examining the tunnel and forgetting to look behind me.|一天，我一边走在前面，一边观察隧道，忘了回头看。
When I stopped, neither my uncle nor Hans answered my call.|等我停下来时，叔叔和汉斯都没有回应我的呼喊。
I tried to find the stream, certain that its water would guide me back.|我试着寻找溪流，深信它能带我回去。
But beneath my hand the ground was dry: I had followed the wrong passage.|可手摸到的地面是干的：我走进了错误的通道。
I hurried, shouted, and hurried again, until the passages seemed to turn around me.|我快走、呼喊，又接着快走，直到周围的通道仿佛都在旋转。
Then my lamp failed, and the darkness became complete.|接着，我的灯灭了，黑暗彻底合拢。
I could not see my own hand, and every frightened thought sounded louder than the last.|我连自己的手也看不见，惊恐的念头却一个比一个响亮。
After a time, a faint voice reached me through the rock; it was my uncle calling.|过了一阵，一个微弱的声音穿过岩石传来；是叔叔在呼唤。
The curved walls carried our voices across a great distance, and we could speak without seeing each other.|弯曲的岩壁把声音传到很远，我们看不见彼此，却能交谈。
I tried to follow his directions, lost my footing on a steep slope, and fell into darkness.|我试着按照他的指点前进，却在陡坡上失足，跌入黑暗。

## A Sea Beneath the Earth|大地之下的海
When I opened my eyes, Hans and my uncle were beside me, and I was safe.|我睁开眼时，汉斯和叔叔就在身边，我得救了。
They had found me after my fall and carried me to a place beyond the tunnels.|他们在我坠落后找到了我，把我带到隧道之外的地方。
A cool wind touched my face, and somewhere nearby I heard waves breaking.|凉风吹到脸上，附近某处传来海浪拍岸的声音。
I thought I must have returned to the surface, but there was no sun above us.|我以为自己已经回到地面，可头顶没有太阳。
An enormous cavern stretched over a sea, lit by a strange brightness high overhead.|一座巨大的洞穴笼罩着海洋，高处奇异的光照亮了四周。
On its shore stood what looked like a forest, though its trunks were giant mushrooms.|岸边像是长着一片森林，可那些树干竟是巨大的蘑菇。
Other plants reminded my uncle of ancient forests whose remains survive in coal.|另一些植物让叔叔想起远古森林，它们的遗存如今保存在煤层里。
He named the water the Lidenbrock Sea, while I wondered how such a world could exist.|他把这片水域命名为利登布洛克海，我却在想，这样的世界怎么可能存在。
Hans began building a raft from the wood he found along the shore.|汉斯开始用岸边找到的木材造筏。
To go farther, we would have to leave solid ground and trust ourselves to this hidden sea.|要继续前进，我们就必须离开坚实的土地，把自己交给这片隐秘的海。

## The Monsters of the Sea|地下海的巨兽
We raised a sail, and the raft moved away from the shore into the pale light.|我们升起帆，木筏离开岸边，驶入苍白的光里。
Hans steered while my uncle measured and recorded, determined to make a map of the unknown.|汉斯掌舵，叔叔测量、记录，决心为未知之地画一张地图。
The fish we caught had no useful eyes; darkness had shaped their lives.|我们捕到的鱼没有能发挥作用的眼睛；黑暗塑造了它们的生活。
Then two enormous shapes appeared, and the sea rose around their moving bodies.|随后，两个庞大的身影出现了，海水随着它们的游动翻涌起来。
For a moment I saw teeth, a long neck, and a body built for powerful swimming.|一瞬间，我看见利齿、长脖子，以及适于强力游动的身躯。
My uncle recognised creatures like the ichthyosaur and the plesiosaur, known to us from fossils.|叔叔认出它们像鱼龙和蛇颈龙，那是我们从化石中认识的动物。
These were sea reptiles, not ordinary fish, and they were fighting each other near our raft.|它们是海生爬行动物，并非普通的鱼，此刻正在木筏附近搏斗。
We could do nothing but watch as waves lifted us, waiting for one blow to smash the wood.|海浪抬起木筏，我们只能眼睁睁地看着，担心哪一次撞击会把木头击碎。
At last the struggle moved away, and the water slowly became quieter.|终于，搏斗渐渐远去，海面慢慢平静下来。
We were still afloat, but I no longer mistook a beautiful discovery for a safe one.|我们仍漂在水上，可我再也不会把美丽的发现误认为安全的发现了。

## The Storm and the Shore|风暴与海岸
Farther on, a great fountain rose from an island, sending hot water into the air.|继续前行时，一座小岛上升起巨大的喷泉，把热水送向半空。
Then the air grew heavy, and dark clouds gathered beneath the cavern roof.|随后，空气变得沉闷，洞顶之下聚起乌云。
A storm struck the sea, and Hans fought to keep the raft from breaking apart.|风暴袭击海面，汉斯竭力不让木筏散架。
A ball of electrical fire appeared near us, terrifying us before it vanished.|一个电光火球出现在附近，把我们吓坏了，随后又消失了。
For a long time the wind drove us helplessly, until the raft crashed onto a shore.|很长一段时间，狂风推着我们漂流，最后木筏撞上了一片海岸。
We were alive, but much of our equipment had suffered, and we did not know exactly where we were.|我们活了下来，可不少装备受了损，我们也不知道自己究竟到了哪里。
My uncle believed we had crossed the sea; later we would discover how badly the compass had misled him.|叔叔相信我们横渡了大海；后来我们才知道，罗盘让他判断得多么离谱。
Walking inland, we found a great field of bones from creatures of the ancient world.|向内陆走去，我们发现遍地都是远古动物的骨骼。
Beyond it, among strange trees, I thought I saw enormous animals and a giant human figure.|再往前，在奇异的树林间，我仿佛看见了巨大的动物和一个巨人般的身影。
We hurried away, shaken by what we thought we had seen and unable to examine it closely.|我们急忙离开，被眼前似乎出现的景象震住了，根本无法靠近查个究竟。

## Saknussemm's Mark|萨克努塞姆的记号
Near a dark passage, we found marks cut into the rock: the initials of Arne Saknussemm.|在一条黑暗通道附近，我们发现刻在岩石上的记号：阿尔内·萨克努塞姆姓名的首字母。
After all our wandering, there was a sign that someone had travelled here before us.|漂流、跋涉了这么久，我们终于看见有人先到过这里的迹象。
My uncle's excitement returned, but a fallen rock blocked the way ahead.|叔叔重新兴奋起来，可一块落石挡住了前路。
He decided to break it with gunpowder, and we moved back onto the raft before the explosion.|他决定用火药炸开它，于是我们在爆炸前退回了木筏。
The blast opened more than a path: the sea poured through the gap into a deep shaft.|爆炸打开的不只是一条路：海水从缺口涌进了一道深井。
The current caught the raft, and we rushed down with the falling water.|急流卷住木筏，我们随着奔腾的水向下冲去。
We could neither turn back nor choose another direction; the whole sea seemed to be chasing us.|我们既不能返回，也无法选择方向；整片海仿佛都在追赶我们。
In the darkness I held on to the wood while Hans kept our few remaining supplies together.|黑暗中，我死死抱住木头，汉斯则护住所剩无几的物资。
At last the movement changed, and the water began carrying us upwards instead of down.|终于，运动的方向变了，水开始载着我们向上，而不再向下。
The heat increased, and my uncle announced that we were inside the passage of an active volcano.|温度升高了，叔叔宣布：我们正处在一座活火山的通道里。

## Back Under the Sun|重见阳光
An eruption was lifting us, and I could scarcely believe my uncle saw a chance of escape in it.|火山喷发正把我们往上推，我几乎不敢相信，叔叔竟把它看作逃生的机会。
Hot water, steam, and broken stones surrounded the raft as it rose through the mountain.|木筏在山体内上升，四周都是热水、蒸汽和碎石。
We clung to it through the terrible movement, unable to control where we would land.|剧烈的颠簸中，我们紧紧抓住它，根本无法决定最后会落在哪里。
Then daylight returned, and we were thrown out onto the side of a mountain.|随后，日光重现，我们被抛到了山坡上。
The air was warm, plants grew nearby, and the sea shone beyond the land.|空气温暖，附近长着植物，陆地之外的大海闪闪发亮。
We had emerged from Stromboli in Italy, far south of the Icelandic volcano where we began.|我们从意大利的斯特龙博利火山出来了，远在出发时的冰岛火山以南。
We had not reached the Earth's centre, whatever my uncle had hoped; our journey had taken another course.|无论叔叔原来怎样期待，我们都没有到达地球中心；旅程走向了另一条路线。
Back in Hamburg, he became famous, while Hans eventually returned to Iceland.|回到汉堡后，叔叔出了名，而汉斯最终回到了冰岛。
I discovered that the storm had reversed the compass needle, explaining why our direction had seemed so certain and been so wrong.|我发现风暴使罗盘的磁针倒转了，这解释了为什么我们对方向如此确信，却又错得如此厉害。
I was glad to see Grauben and my home again, carrying memories of a sea no ordinary map could show.|我高兴地再见到格劳本和家，也带回了一片普通地图无法标出的海的记忆。

## After the Adventure|探险之后
This is a science fiction adventure, not a true report of travel inside the Earth.|这是一部科幻冒险故事，并不是真实的地球内部旅行报告。
Real rocks and fossils help scientists study the past, but the underground sea and surviving ancient reptiles belong to the novel.|真实的岩石与化石帮助科学家研究过去，但地下海和仍然存活的远古爬行动物属于小说世界。
The Earth becomes extremely hot deep below the surface, and a volcano is not a safe entrance to its centre.|地表深处的地球温度极高，火山并不是通向地心的安全入口。
Axel often feels afraid, yet he keeps observing, asking questions, and trying to understand what happens.|阿克塞尔常常害怕，却仍不断观察、提问，努力理解发生的一切。
Hans helps the group through practical skills, while the professor gives them the determination to explore.|汉斯用实际技能帮助大家，教授则给了他们探索的决心。
Whose action mattered most to you, and when would you have asked the group to turn back?|你觉得谁的行动最重要？如果是你，会在什么时候提出返回？
