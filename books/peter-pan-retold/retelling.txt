The Open Window
Wendy Darling lived in London with her brothers, John and Michael.
Their dog, Nana, helped look after them.
At night, Wendy told stories about a boy called Peter Pan.
He could fly, and he did not want to grow up.
One night, Peter came through the bedroom window.
A tiny fairy called Tinker Bell came with him.
Peter was looking for his shadow.
Nana had caught it when he visited the room before.
He found it, but he could not join it to his feet.
Wendy woke up and saw him crying on the floor.
She carefully sewed his shadow back on.
Peter jumped up and said that he had done it himself.
Wendy was hurt, and he quickly tried to make her smile.
They gave each other small gifts.
Peter gave Wendy an acorn button, which she wore on a chain.
Then he told her about an island called Neverland.
The Lost Boys lived there, but they had no one to tell them stories.
Peter asked Wendy to come with him.

Across the Night Sky
Wendy wanted an adventure, but she would not leave her brothers behind.
Peter agreed to take all three children.
He taught them to think happy thoughts.
Tinker Bell gave them fairy dust, and their feet left the floor.
Soon they were flying above the roofs of London.
The streets below looked like thin lines of light.
Wendy felt excited when the city disappeared behind them.
They flew over the sea for a long time.
The children became tired and hungry, but Peter kept playing.
Sometimes he flew away and almost forgot to come back.
Wendy began to see that flying was easier for him than caring for others.
At last, dark trees and bright water appeared below.
There were mountains, a small lake, and a pirate ship.
The island looked like a dream that had become real.
Then a gun fired from the ship.
The children rushed into the clouds and lost sight of one another.
Tinker Bell offered to guide Wendy to a safe place.
Wendy followed her little light toward the trees.

The Arrow in the Forest
Tinker Bell was jealous because Peter liked talking to Wendy.
She flew ahead and found the Lost Boys.
She told them that Peter wanted them to shoot the strange flying girl.
One boy, Tootles, believed her and let an arrow fly.
Wendy fell to the ground, and the boys hurried toward her.
When Peter arrived, he was angry and afraid.
Then he saw the acorn button hanging from Wendy's chain.
The arrow had struck the button instead of her heart.
Wendy was alive, and she slowly opened her eyes.
Tootles was sorry and offered Peter his life.
Wendy stopped Peter from hurting him.
Peter sent Tinker Bell away for a time because of her cruel trick.
The boys built a small house around Wendy while she rested.
They hoped that she would stay and look after them.
Wendy said she was only a girl and did not know much about being a mother.
The boys said that she already knew more than they did.
Peter stood outside with his sword and kept watch.
For that night, Wendy and her brothers were safe.

A Home Under the Ground
The children moved into a secret home under the ground.
Each boy climbed down through a hole inside a tree.
There was a table, a warm fire, and a large bed.
Tinker Bell had a tiny room of her own.
Wendy told stories and helped the boys mend their clothes.
John and Michael joined the games and quickly felt at home.
Sometimes the children pretended to have a wonderful dinner.
Peter enjoyed the game even when there was little real food.
He called himself their father, but he meant it only as a game.
Wendy sometimes wished he could understand her feelings more clearly.
Peter was brave and full of ideas, but he could be careless.
He loved exciting stories and often forgot what had happened yesterday.
The other children began to forget parts of their old home, too.
Wendy asked them questions about their parents so they would remember.
At night, she wondered whether her mother was still waiting.
Outside, the island was beautiful, but it was never completely safe.
The pirates were looking for Peter and the Lost Boys.
Their captain wanted revenge.

The Captain and the Clock
Captain Hook had a sharp metal hook instead of one hand.
Peter had cut off that hand during an earlier fight.
A crocodile had eaten it and wanted to catch Hook again.
The crocodile had also swallowed a clock.
Its ticking sound warned Hook whenever it came near.
The captain feared that sound more than any sword.
But one day the clock might stop.
Then the crocodile could come without a warning.
Hook was proud of his fine coat and careful manners.
He wanted people to think he was better than everyone else.
Peter's laughter made him furious.
With his pirate Smee, Hook searched the forest for the secret home.
They found the trees with holes inside them.
Hook began making a plan to catch the children.
Suddenly, a slow ticking came through the trees.
Hook forgot his plan and ran away.
Peter was not the only danger waiting for him on the island.

The Rising Water
One day, Peter and Wendy visited the mermaids' lake.
The mermaids played in the water but were not kind to Wendy.
Soon a pirate boat arrived with Tiger Lily tied inside it.
She belonged to a community that lived on the island.
The pirates left her on a rock as the water rose.
Peter hid nearby and copied Hook's voice.
He ordered the pirates to let Tiger Lily go.
They were afraid to argue with their captain, so they obeyed.
Then the real Hook arrived, and a fight began.
Peter was hurt while trying to fight fairly.
The water kept rising, and Wendy could not fly away.
Peter helped her escape on a large kite.
He stayed behind on the rock as the sea covered it.
For once, there was no easy game that could save him.
A bird saw him and pushed her floating nest toward him.
Peter moved the bird's eggs into a hat to keep them safe.
Then he climbed into the nest and floated away.
That night, Wendy was grateful to see him come home.

Wendy's Choice
Wendy told the boys a story about three children who flew away.
In the story, their mother kept the window open until they returned.
The boys liked this happy ending, but Peter grew quiet.
He said that he had once gone back to his own home.
He believed the window was closed and another child was in his bed.
No one knew whether his memory was right.
But Wendy understood that Peter was afraid of being forgotten.
She suddenly wanted to see her parents.
John and Michael wanted to go home, too.
The Lost Boys feared losing Wendy and asked her to stay.
She offered to take them home and find them a family.
Peter acted as if he did not care, but he was unhappy.
Some boys wanted to stop Wendy from leaving.
Peter would not keep her against her wishes.
The children prepared for their journey and said goodbye.
They climbed out through the trees while Peter stayed below.
Above them, the pirates were waiting in the dark.
The journey home had become a trap.

A Small Light
The pirates attacked the island guards and caught the children.
They tied Wendy and the boys and carried them to the ship.
Below the ground, Peter was still asleep.
Hook climbed down into the secret home without making a sound.
He put poison into Peter's cup of medicine and slipped away.
Tinker Bell returned and woke Peter.
She told him what had happened to the children.
Peter reached for his medicine before going to save them.
Tinker Bell warned him, but he did not believe her.
Before he could drink, she drank the poison herself.
Her bright light became weak, and she fell beside him.
Peter finally understood what she had done for him.
She said she could live if children still believed in fairies.
Peter called into the night for anyone who could hear him.
Far away, children clapped their hands to show their belief.
Tinker Bell's light slowly became bright again.
Peter thanked her, then took his sword.
Together, they flew toward the pirate ship.

The Ship in the Dark
On the ship, Hook asked the boys to join his crew.
They refused, so he ordered them to walk off a narrow board into the sea.
Wendy told them to be brave, although she was afraid herself.
Then a ticking sound came over the water.
Hook thought the crocodile was near and hid in fear.
But it was Peter, copying the sound of the clock.
He climbed onto the ship while the pirates were confused.
He slipped into a dark room below the deck.
One pirate entered the room and did not come back.
The other pirates became afraid of something they could not see.
Peter reached the children and quietly cut their ropes.
They hid while he went to find Hook.
Wendy waited for a sign that it was safe to move.
She knew that being quiet could matter as much as fighting.
At last, Peter stood in front of the captain.
There was no more hiding and no more ticking trick.
Hook raised his sword, and Peter raised his own.

Hook or Peter
The two enemies fought across the deck.
Hook was strong and knew many cruel tricks.
Peter was quick and could fly out of danger.
When Hook lost his sword, Peter let him pick it up.
This made the proud captain even more angry.
He could not bear kindness from the boy he hated.
Around them, the children fought the other pirates.
Soon Peter and his friends were winning.
Hook tried one last trick, but Peter stopped him.
The captain jumped from the ship into the sea.
The crocodile was waiting below.
Hook never came back.
Peter put on the captain's coat and began giving orders.
For a moment, he looked very pleased to have power.
Wendy reminded him that they still had a journey to finish.
The children set the ship's course toward home.
The adventure was ending, but Wendy's biggest choice was still ahead.

The Way Home
Peter reached the Darling house before the other children.
He thought about closing the bedroom window.
If Wendy thought her mother had forgotten her, she might return to Neverland.
Then Peter saw Mrs Darling sitting beside the empty beds.
She looked sad and was still waiting for her children.
Peter changed his mind and left the window open.
Wendy, John, and Michael flew in and climbed into their beds.
At first, their mother thought she was dreaming.
Then she heard their voices and held them in her arms.
Mr Darling and Nana came running into the room.
The Lost Boys waited nearby, hoping there would be room for them.
The Darlings welcomed them into the family.
Mrs Darling offered Peter a home, too.
But Peter asked whether he would have to go to school and grow up.
When she said yes, he refused.
Wendy wanted to stay with her family, even though she loved flying.
Peter promised to return for her again.
He flew away, leaving Wendy beside the open window.

When Wendy Grew Up
The Lost Boys went to school and slowly became part of ordinary life.
Wendy grew older with her brothers.
Peter sometimes returned, but he did not come every year.
For him, one adventure quickly replaced another.
Wendy remembered things that Peter had already forgotten.
One day, Wendy was grown up and had a daughter called Jane.
She told Jane stories about Neverland and the boy who could fly.
Then Peter came through the window again.
He was still a boy, but Wendy was no longer a child.
Peter did not understand how so much time could have passed.
He sat on the floor and cried.
Jane woke up and asked him what was wrong.
Soon Peter was teaching her to fly.
Wendy let Jane visit Neverland for a little while.
She could no longer fly with Peter, but she could still love the story.
Later, Jane would tell it to her own daughter.
Peter's adventures would begin again, with another child beside him.
Wendy's adventure had changed her, and she carried it into the life she chose.
