A Monster in the Sea
In 1866, ships began meeting something strange at sea.
It was long, fast, and powerful enough to break a ship.
Some people thought it was a giant animal that science had never seen.
Professor Aronnax studied sea animals, so he joined a ship sent to find it.
His careful servant, Conseil, went with him.
On board they met Ned Land, a skilled hunter from Canada.
Aronnax wanted knowledge, but Ned wanted something he could catch.
For months they searched the empty water.
Then, one night, a bright light moved beneath the waves.
The ship followed it, and Ned threw his sharp weapon.
Instead of sinking into flesh, it struck something hard.
The strange object hit back, throwing Aronnax into the sea.

The Man Called Nemo
Conseil jumped after his master, and the two men struggled in the dark.
Ned had also fallen overboard, but he had found a solid place to stand.
The monster was made of metal.
It was a submarine, a ship that could travel under water.
A door opened, and men pulled the three strangers inside.
Their captain called himself Nemo, a name meaning nobody.
His ship was the Nautilus, and electricity gave it light and power.
He had books, paintings, and a great window into the sea.
Nemo offered his guests food and safety, but not the right to leave.
They knew his secret, and he would not let that secret reach the world.
Aronnax was excited by the science, yet troubled by this rule.
Could a beautiful home still be a prison?

Walking Beneath the Waves
The Nautilus crossed the Pacific Ocean.
Through its window, Aronnax saw fish shining like living jewels.
Nemo showed him forests growing on the sea floor.
Wearing special suits and carrying air, they walked where no ordinary person could breathe.
Above them, sunlight passed through the water like green glass.
The sea seemed like another world, full of beauty and danger.
Aronnax wrote down what he saw, afraid that he might forget.
Ned kept looking for chances to escape.
He needed the open air and the freedom to choose his own road.
The friends began to want different things.
For Aronnax, each new discovery made leaving harder.
But learning a secret did not make losing freedom harmless.

A Grave and a Pearl Diver
Near an island, the submarine became stuck in shallow water.
Ned enjoyed a brief trip onto land, but the men had to return.
Later, Aronnax was asked to help a badly injured sailor.
He could not save the man.
Nemo took his crew to a quiet graveyard beneath the sea.
The captain's sadness showed how deeply he cared for his people.
In the Indian Ocean, they saw a poor man diving for pearls.
A shark moved toward him, and Nemo rushed to help.
Ned helped kill the shark, saving Nemo as well as the diver.
Nemo gave the man a bag of pearls.
Aronnax saw kindness in this secretive captain.
Still, kindness in one moment could not answer every question about him.

Roads No Map Could Show
Nemo led them through a hidden passage beneath the land.
The submarine moved from the Red Sea toward the Mediterranean.
Aronnax learned that the sea held roads missing from ordinary maps.
He also saw Nemo send gold to people fighting for freedom.
The captain had not truly forgotten the world above.
One night, Nemo showed Aronnax ruined buildings beneath the Atlantic.
He said they were the remains of Atlantis.
In this adventure, an ancient lost city lived beneath the waves.
Ned cared less about ruined cities than about reaching home.
The more they traveled, the farther home seemed to move away.
Aronnax could understand his friend, even when curiosity held him back.
No discovery belonged only to the person who controlled the door.

Under the Ice
The Nautilus traveled south, into a world of ice.
Nemo wanted to reach the South Pole by going beneath the frozen sea.
For a short time, his bold plan seemed successful.
Then a huge piece of ice turned over, trapping the submarine.
Ice closed around them, and their fresh air began to run out.
Science had brought them here, but it did not make them safe from nature.
The crew worked together, cutting a path through the ice.
Inside the ship, the men grew weak and struggled to breathe.
Conseil and Ned shared what little air they could with Aronnax.
At last, the Nautilus broke free and rose into open water.
Aronnax breathed deeply, grateful for something he usually took for granted.
He had learned more about friendship than any list of sea animals could teach.

The Captain's Darkness
Farther north, giant sea creatures attacked the submarine.
Their long arms held the ship, and the crew fought on the deck.
Nemo saved Ned, but another sailor was carried away.
Afterward, the captain sat in sorrow.
Then a warship appeared and fired at the Nautilus.
Nemo said its people had destroyed his country and his family.
Aronnax begged him not to answer suffering with more suffering.
Nemo refused and drove his submarine into the warship.
Aronnax watched the ship sink, with people still on board.
The captain who had saved a poor diver could also destroy many lives.
His pain was real, but it did not make his revenge right.
Aronnax knew that he could no longer remain simply to learn.

Freedom in a Storm
The Nautilus moved into the cold waters near Norway.
Ned found a chance to take a small boat attached to the submarine.
This time, Aronnax agreed to go.
Before leaving, he heard Nemo speak words of grief in the darkness.
He felt pity, but pity could not require him to remain a prisoner.
The three friends climbed into the boat as a terrible whirlpool caught the ship.
Water spun around them, and Aronnax lost consciousness.
When he woke, he was safe in a fisherman's home with Conseil and Ned.
He did not know whether Nemo or the Nautilus had survived.
Their journey had covered twenty thousand leagues through the seas.
That was the distance traveled, not a depth beneath the surface.
Aronnax could now share his discoveries, while still wondering about the man who had shown them to him.
